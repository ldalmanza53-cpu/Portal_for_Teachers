Grammar Rule Overview

How Questions & Short Answers

In English, we use different question phrases starting with How to ask about frequency, duration, and skills or abilities. Tap any card below to listen to standard pronunciation!

Frequency

How often...

Asks about how many times or how repeatedly an action happens.

"How often do you work out?"

Every day.
Twice a week.
Not very often.
Formula: How often + do/does + subject + verb?
Duration / Time

How long...

Asks about length of time or duration spent doing something.

"How long do you spend at the gym?"

Thirty minutes a day.
Two hours a week.
About an hour on weekends.
Formula: How long + do/does + subject + spend/stay?
Skill + Verb

How well...

Asks about ability/skill performing an action verb.

"How well do you play tennis?"

Pretty well.
About average.
Not very well.
Formula: How well + do/does + subject + action verb?
Skill + "be at"

How good...

Asks about overall ability using the adjective structure be good at.

"How good are you at sports?"

Pretty good.
OK.
Not so good.
Formula: How good + be + subject + at + noun/-ing?

Key Grammar Contrast: "How well" vs. "How good"

Notice how the action word structure changes depending on whether you use well or good at.

Option 1: How well + Verb Adverb Rule

Use well directly with an action verb (play, swim, cook, speak):

How well + do you + swim?
➜ I swim pretty well.
Option 2: How good + be + at Preposition Rule

Use good with are you at + noun or verb-ing:

How good + are you at + swimming / sports?
➜ I'm pretty good at it.
