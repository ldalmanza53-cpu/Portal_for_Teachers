🪄 The Magic-"E" Transformer: Short CVC vs. Long Diphthong CVCe

When a silent -e jumps onto the end of a CVC word, it transforms the short vowel into a long diphthong (/æ/ → /eɪ/, /ɪ/ → /aɪ/, /ɒ/ → /oʊ/, /ʌ/ → /juː/) while keeping the final consonant crisp.

💡 Ayuda ES: Cuando la "e" muda aparece al final (Magic E), la vocal anterior cambia de un sonido corto (aplauso seco) a un sonido largo o diptongo (estiramiento de banda elástica).

🚪 The 3 Doors of "-ED" & Beatbox Rhythm Lab

Golden Rule: Only verbs ending in a T or D sound add an extra syllable (/ɪd/). Verbs ending in voiceless sounds (/t/ Pop) or voiced sounds (/d/ Hum) add ZERO extra syllables!

💡 Ayuda ES: Solo los verbos que terminan en sonido T o D agregan una sílaba extra (/ɪd/). En verbos como "walked" o "played", la "-ed" se pega sin agregar sílabas (1 solo aplauso).

🎛️ The 3 Doors Acoustic Reference & Beatbox Relay Chant Player

Door 1: /t/ POP (Voiceless)

After k, p, s, sh, ch, f • 0 Extra Syllables (1 Clap)

Door 2: /d/ HUM (Voiced)

After l, n, r, v, g, b, z, vowels • 0 Extra Syllables (1 Clap)

Door 3: /ɪd/ BOUNCE (After T/D)

ONLY after T or D sounds • +1 Extra Syllable (2+ Claps)

⚡ Flap-T (/ɾ/), Glottal-T (/ʔ/) & Palatalization Lab

Master North American allophonic processes: rapid alveolar tapping (Flap-T /ɾ/), throat catches before syllabic /n/ (Glottal-T /ʔ/), and cross-word palatalization (t/d + y → tʃ / dʒ).

💡 Ayuda ES: En inglés norteamericano, la T entre vocales suena como una "r" suave española (Flap-T /ɾ/), antes de "-ain/-on" se corta en la garganta (Glottal-T /ʔ/), y al unirse con "you" se fusiona en "didja" (/dʒ/) o "doncha" (/tʃ/).

🎛️ American Allophony & Palatalization Soundboard (Click to Compare)

1. Flap-T (/ɾ/ — Quick Spanish 'r' tap)

Intervocalic /t/ or /d/ before an unstressed vowel taps rapidly against the alveolar ridge.

2. Glottal-T (/ʔ/ — Throat Catch)

Before syllabic /n/ (-tain, -ton), the tongue stays still while the glottis briefly closes.

3. Palatalization (/d+j → dʒ/, /t+j → tʃ/)

Across word boundaries before "you", /d/ becomes /dʒ/ and /t/ becomes /tʃ/.
