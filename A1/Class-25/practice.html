Adverbs of Frequency & Questions with How

Master sentence word order for frequency adverbs and learn when to use How good vs. How well!

1

Adverbs of Frequency

Position in sentences

Before main verbs vs. After verb be

Adverbs (always, almost always, usually, often, sometimes, hardly ever, almost never, never) come before the main verb, but after be.

She never plays tennis.
I almost always eat breakfast.
I'm always late. (after verb be)

Sentence Starters

Usually and Sometimes can begin a sentence:

"Usually I walk to work. Sometimes I exercise in the morning."

End-of-Sentence Expressions

Expressions like every day, once a week, twice a month, three times a year go at the end:

"Do you exercise every day? I exercise three times a week."

2

Questions with How

Short answers & Good vs. Well

Don't confuse GOOD and WELL!

Use the adjective good with the verb be. Use the adverb well with other action verbs.

How good are you at... + [noun/activity]?

Question: How good are you at soccer?

Answer: Pretty good! / Not very good.

How well do you... + [verb]?

Question: How well do you play soccer?

Answer: Not very well, but I love it.

How often / How long

How often: Frequency (e.g., "Four times a week.")

How long: Duration/Time (e.g., "About six hours a week.")
