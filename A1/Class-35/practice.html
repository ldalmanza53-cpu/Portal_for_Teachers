Unit 9: Describing a Neighborhood (Much vs. Many)

MUCH + Noncount Nouns

• Singular concepts that cannot be counted individually.

• Examples: crime, street noise, shopping, traffic, pollution.

MANY + Count Nouns (Plural)

• Plural items that can be counted individually.

• Examples: roads, students, parking spaces, shops, parks.

Part A: Select Many or Much

Choose the correct quantifier for each blank in the conversation.

A: Is there crime in this neighborhood?

B: Oh no. There isn't crime at all. Not anymore.

A: How about noise? How street noise is there?

B: Well, there aren't major roads in this neighborhood.

A: Are there students in the neighborhood?

B: Yes. It's very close to the university, so students live here.

A: I see. How parking spaces come with the apartment?

B: I'm afraid there aren't any parking spaces. You have to park on the street.

A: How about supermarkets? Is there shopping nearby?

B: Oh, yes. There's a big supermarket just down the street. And there are other shops nearby, too.
