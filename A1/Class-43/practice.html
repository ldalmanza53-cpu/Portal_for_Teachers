Grammar Rule

1. Present Perfect; already, yet

Present Perfect: Actions that happened some time in the past (indefinite time).

"I've checked my email."

YET: Questions & negative statements (end of clause).

"Have you checked your email yet?" / "I haven't turned on my computer yet."

ALREADY: Affirmative statements (between have/has and past participle, or end of sentence).

"I've already checked my email."

Grammar Rule

2. Present Perfect vs. Simple Past

PRESENT PERFECT Indefinite / Unspecified time

Don't mention a specific time!

"I've been to a jazz club."

SIMPLE PAST Specific finished time

Use simple past for specific past times (e.g. last night, yesterday, 2 years ago)!

"I went to a jazz club last night."

Grammar Rule

3. For and Since

FOR + Period of Time Duration / Length

Describes how long a condition has been true.

"We've been in New York for two months." (arrived 2 months ago)

SINCE + Point in Time Starting Moment

Describes when a condition started in the past.

"We've been here since August." (from August until now)

⏳ Reference: Common Time Expressions

FOR (Duration)
  • for almost 10 years
  • for six months
  • for a long time
  • for three days / two weeks / five hours
SINCE (Start Point)
  • since March
  • since August / 2018 / yesterday
  • since elementary school
  • since I was a kid
