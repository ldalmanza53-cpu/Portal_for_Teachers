Real Context & Scenario

The Local Housing & Welcome Exchange

Students are participating in a Community Center Housing Exchange. Local residents who know their neighborhood well meet with new arrivals ("House Hunters") who want to find a good area to move into. They exchange clear information about local facilities, safety, transport, and overall atmosphere.

Role A

The House Hunter / New Resident

  • Ask questions to learn about facilities, services, and neighborhood safety.
  • Use target question frames: "Is there a... near your home?", "Are there any...?", "What is your neighborhood like?"
  • Record details in your Partner Profile Log tab to summarize at the end.
Target Key Prompt: "Is there a bakery next to the bus stop? Is it quiet at night?"
Role B

The Local Resident

  • Describe your real or imagined neighborhood in detail.
  • Use existential structures: "There is a...", "There are two...", "There isn't a..."
  • Give clear locations using prepositions: next to, behind, opposite, between, near.
Target Key Response: "My neighborhood is quiet. There is a small bakery next to my building."

"There is" vs. "There are" Guide

Type Structure Example
Singular (+) There is a / an... There is a quiet bakery.
Plural (+) There are (two / some)... There are two parks.
Singular (-) There isn't a... There isn't a gym.
Plural (-) There aren't any... There aren't any malls.
Question (?) Is there a...? Is there a bus stop near here?

Prepositions of Place Matrix

next to The bakery is next to the bank.
opposite / across from The school is opposite the park.
behind The parking lot is behind the building.
between The cafe is between two stores.
near / close to There is a stop close to my house.
far from The airport is far from the center.

Neighborhood Adjectives

Positive Features
  • Quiet & peaceful
  • Clean & safe
  • Friendly neighbors
  • Convenient & modern
  • Green (lots of trees)
Busy / Challenging
  • Busy & noisy
  • Crowded streets
  • Dark at night
  • Old buildings
  • Far from downtown

House Hunter Question Frames

  • Atmosphere: "What is your neighborhood like?"
  • Specific Place: "Is there a [bakery / gym / pharmacy] near your home?"
  • Plural Check: "Are there any [parks / restaurants] in your area?"
  • Location Check: "Where is the bus stop located?"
