Expressing Cultural Contrasts & Exceptions

When talking about your home country, avoid absolute generalizations! Use **contrast linkers** to highlight how things differ from other countries, and **exception phrases** to mention special cases.

1. Contrast Markers

Used to highlight differences between two facts

Followed by a Full Clause (Subject + Verb) whereas / while

"In my country people eat lunch as the main meal, whereas in North America people usually prefer a light lunch."

Followed by a Noun / Noun Phrase unlike / in contrast to

"Unlike many fast-paced modern cities, people in my hometown still enjoy taking a short afternoon break."

Starting a New Sentence On the other hand, ...

"Many European countries have four distinct weather seasons. On the other hand, my tropical country stays warm all year long."

2. Exception Markers

Used to mention special cases or limitations

Followed by a Noun / Gerund (-ing) except for / apart from

"Except for national holidays, small family-run grocery shops remain open every day of the week."

Formal Noun Exception with the exception of

"Public transport is usually very reliable, with the exception of high-mountain bus routes during heavy rains."

Followed by Condition / Situation except when / except where

"Buses generally run without fixed schedules, except when they leave the central terminal in the morning."

Grammar Summary Cheat Sheet

  • whereas / while: Contrast + Full Clause (Subject + Verb)
  • unlike / in contrast to: Contrast + Noun Phrase
  • except for / apart from / with the exception of: Exception + Noun / Gerund (-ing)
  • except when: Exception + Condition / Clause
