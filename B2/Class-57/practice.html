Unit T-71 Grammar Focus

Clauses and Phrases Showing Contrast and Exception

Learn how to contrast two distinct facts and highlight exceptions using B1 structures: while, unlike, in contrast to, except (that), except (for), and except for the fact (that).

Showing Contrast

1. while + Clause (Subject + Verb)

"While the typical Italian person studies until 22, the typical Japanese person studies until 24."

2. unlike + Noun Phrase / Pronoun

"Unlike the Japanese, Italians seem to drink a lot of bottled water."

3. in contrast to + Noun Phrase / Pronoun

"In contrast to Italians, the Japanese travel a lot more by rail."

Showing Exception

1. except (that) + Clause

"Both like TV, except (that) the typical Italian person watches more."

2. except (for) + Noun Phrase

"Italian and Japanese people are fairly different, except (for) the age they get married."

3. except for the fact (that) + Clause

"Japanese people consume less, except for the fact (that) they drink more tea."

Exercise 2A Question & Classification Guide

Notice the sentences in Part 1B that use phrases with except. Which phrase is followed by a clause?

Answer: except (that) and except for the fact (that) are followed by full clauses (Subject + Verb). In contrast, except (for) is followed by a noun phrase or noun clause without a main conjugated verb.
