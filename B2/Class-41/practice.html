Adverbs with Simple Past and Past Perfect

Master how to express time relationships in narrative stories! Use the Simple Past for sequential main events and the Past Perfect (had + past participle) for completed actions that happened earlier in time.

WHEN

Time Clause Sequence

Sequential vs. Earlier Completed Actions

When with simple past and past perfect expresses clear time ordering:

Prior Action: "When I arrived in Bangkok, my connecting flight had already departed."

→ Flight departed FIRST, then I arrived.

Sequential Action: "When I arrived in Bangkok, my friend met me at the airport."

→ Both happened in sequence.

BEFORE

Sequence Clarity

Simple Past or Past Perfect

When before makes the order obvious, either tense can be used:

"It began to rain before she boarded the plane."

"It had begun to rain before she boarded the plane."

→ Both forms are grammatically correct!

YET / ALREADY

Prior Event Markers

Events completed before a past point

Already and yet emphasize earlier past occurrences:

"It had already started raining when I arrived in Bangkok."

"It hadn't started raining yet when I arrived in Bangkok."
