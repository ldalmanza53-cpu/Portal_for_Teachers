Andrew (lived in Paris, France):

"When I first moved to Paris, I admired the stunning old buildings and rich history, but I didn't know anyone. What really helped me get used to my new life was joining a local soccer team. Having fun playing a sport allowed me to meet friends naturally. However, I found it difficult to adjust to how late businesses closed or how fast people spoke!"

Rachel (lived in Mexico City, Mexico):

"Living in Mexico was amazing! What helped me adjust was sharing late-night meals with local friends—we enjoyed food together and talked for hours. But at first, I felt people talked about themselves a lot in conversation. So I tried to talk about myself more to fit the style. It was also hard to adjust to eating dinner so late at night, around 9:00 or 10:00 p.m.!"

Layla (lived in Tokyo, Japan):

"In Tokyo, I didn't feel connected to others at first and felt very homesick. The language and social customs were so different. What saved me was watching Japanese comedy shows on TV every evening! It helped me understand local humor, practice my listening skills, and feel less lonely until I made closer friends."

Writing Conclusions: Techniques & Linking Words

Master how to close compositions effectively and link supporting details seamlessly.

The 4 Core Methods to Conclude a Composition

A strong conclusion wraps up your writing effectively. You can close your composition by:

1. Restating Restating the main idea in fresh words.
2. Summarizing Summarizing key supporting points.
3. Looking Ahead Looking to the future impact or outcome.
4. Recommending Making final recommendations or judgments.

Paragraph 1: Gap Year Travel vs. Higher Education

Overall, for the majority of young people, taking a year off to travel has not replaced the traditional postsecondary pursuit of higher education. For most high school graduates, studying or training for a career will remain the most likely path. However, there are still many young people who save as much money as they can in order to see foreign sights and experience foreign cultures. Taking time to travel may put them behind a year as far as college and careers are concerned, but they say the personal growth is worth it.

Paragraph 2: Benefits of Studying Abroad

In brief, there are real benefits to studying abroad. While it is certainly possible to learn the language in a country where it is not widely spoken, living abroad offers limitless possibilities for improvement. Additionally, foreign students have a real opportunity to hone their life skills. Generally, they are responsible for everything from money management, accommodations, and meals to ensuring that they have a good balance between their social life and school life. Studying abroad offers not only language lessons, but also life lessons, and is well worth considering.
