Relative Clauses & Pronoun Omission

Learn when relative pronouns (who, that, which) are required and when they can be omitted (dropped)!

Subject Relative Clauses

Pronoun is followed directly by a VERB

Required

When the relative pronoun acts as the subject of the relative clause (followed directly by a verb), you CANNOT omit the pronoun.

Example 1 (People):

People who / that [chew] gum loudly get on my nerves.

❌ Cannot say: "People chew gum loudly get on my nerves."

Example 2 (Things):

One thing that / which [makes] me sick is selfish people.

Object Relative Clauses

Pronoun is followed by SUBJECT + VERB

Optional (Omit)

When the relative pronoun acts as the object of the relative clause (followed by a subject noun/pronoun), you CAN omit the pronoun (represented as Ø / X).

Example 1 (With Pronoun):

The restaurant (that) [we] had dinner at overcharged us.

Example 2 (Pronoun Omitted - Natural B1 Speaking):

The movie [Ø] I saw last night was fantastic.

✓ Both versions are 100% correct!
