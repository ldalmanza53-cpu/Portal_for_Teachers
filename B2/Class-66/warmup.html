Rule 1: Repetitive Habits

Continuous with Always/Constantly

Uses present continuous with frequency adverbs to signal irritation rather than routine.

Subject + is/are + always/constantly + V-ing

"He is always tapping his pen on the desk."

Rule 2: Dummy Subject

It + Action Verb + Clause

Shifts focus to the frustrating action or scenario using 'It' as an introductory pronoun.

It + [bugs / drives me crazy] + when + [Clause]

"It bugs me when people talk loudly on speakerphone."

Rule 3: Cleft Sentence

What Clause Focus

Emphasizes the exact source of annoyance for dramatic or stylistic impact.

What + [annoys / bothers] me most + is + [Clause]

"What annoys me most is when drivers fail to indicate."

Diplomatic English Checklist:
  • Depersonalize: Blame the situation, not the person (*"Noise levels were high"* vs *"You are noisy"*).
  • Softening Modals: Use *could*, *would*, *it seems*, *appears to be*.
  • Solution-Oriented: Propose a clear, polite next step.
