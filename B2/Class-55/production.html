Your Role: Junior News Commentator & Cultural Critic.
Your Mission: React to breaking news, workplace situations, and social events live on air. To express professional opinions and comments about a full situation, you will use non-defining relative clauses with "which" as sentence modifiers.

Grammar Formula & Structure

When which follows a comma, it can modify an entire sentence or situation rather than just a single noun!

// Sentence Modifier Formula
[ Complete Event / Fact ] , which [ Verb Phrase Comment ]
Broadcast Example:
"The train arrived 30 minutes early, which surprised all the commuters."
👉 What surprised everyone? Not just 'the train' or '30 minutes', but the entire fact that it arrived early.
Useful Commentary Phrases (B1 Toolkit)

Use these ready-to-go comment clauses to react quickly in your broadcasts:

, which was unexpected.
, which made everyone happy.
, which caused a massive delay.
, which surprised the board.
, which proved to be a mistake.
, which delighted the fans.

Critical B1 Mistakes to Avoid

1. Using "that" after a comma
❌ ...early, that surprised us.
✅ ...early, which surprised us.

Non-defining relative clauses modifying whole sentences must use which, never that.

2. Forgetting the Comma
❌ ...early which surprised us.
✅ ...early, which surprised us.

The comma is compulsory to signal that you are commenting on the whole previous clause!

3. Comma Splice ("it")
❌ ...early, it surprised us.
✅ ...early, which surprised us.

You need the relative pronoun which to link the two clauses together seamlessly.
