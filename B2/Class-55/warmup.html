Sentence-Modifying Relative Clauses (", which")

⚡

Grammar Core Rule

Commenting on Whole Sentences with ", which"

In English, a non-defining relative clause starting with ", which" can refer back to an entire situation, action, or idea in the preceding main clause—not just a single noun!

Noun-Modifying Clause 🎒

Here, which modifies only one specific noun immediately before the comma.

He bought a watch, which was very expensive.
💡 What was expensive? Just the watch (the noun).
Sentence-Modifying Clause 🌐

Here, which modifies the entire main clause / event.

He lost his watch, which ruined his entire week.
💡 What ruined his week? The whole situation of losing his watch, not the watch itself.

🔬 Sentence Syntax Anatomy Visualizer

The train was delayed by two hours , which ruined all our weekend plans.
1. Main Clause (Fact)

Complete independent sentence expressing the situation.

2. Essential Comma

Crucial pause separator! Never omit this comma.

3. Pronoun ("which")

Refers to the entire preceding clause as a single idea.

4. Comment / Consequence

Your reaction, consequence, evaluation, or result.

Test the 3 Critical Grammar Rules

Click the option variations below to test what happens when learners make common grammar mistakes:

She won the global science competition , which surprised everyone. , that surprised everyone. , what surprised everyone. which surprised everyone.

🎉 Perfect Grammar!

The comma creates a non-defining pause, and which acts as a pronoun representing the whole fact ("She won the global science competition").

🛑 Trap #1: "that" after a comma

Never use "that" after a comma to make a sentence comment! "That" is only used in defining relative clauses without commas.

🛑 Trap #2: Using "what" to link clauses

"what" cannot connect two clauses like this. "What" means "the thing that" and includes its own antecedent (e.g. "I heard what you said.").

⚠️ Trap #3: Missing Comma

Without a comma, the sentence looks like a restrictive defining clause. Sentence-modifying clauses MUST always have a comma before "which".
