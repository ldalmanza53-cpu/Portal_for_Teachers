B1 Giving Opinions Using Questions Guide

1. Negative Questions to Ask for Opinions

In B1 conversation, starting questions with negative auxiliary verbs politely invites agreement while proposing an opinion:

  • Don't you think that smartphone bans help students concentrate?
  • Wouldn't you agree that social media creates unnecessary pressure?
  • Don't you feel that AI tools can make learning more engaging?

2. Tag Questions for Agreement & Balance

Attach short tag questions at the end of statements to check co-host agreement and keep the discussion moving smoothly:

  • Strict phone bans are hard to enforce, aren't they?
  • Digital detox improves student focus, doesn't it?
  • We should allow devices during lunch breaks, shouldn't we?

3. Clear Stance Answers & Responses

Respond to opinion questions clearly using structured B1 openers:

  • In my view, full smartphone bans create a disciplined environment.
  • From my perspective, teaching digital responsibility is better than a total ban.
  • I completely agree with you, but we must also consider teacher workload.
