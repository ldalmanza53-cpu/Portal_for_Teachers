Pillar 1

Degrees of Certainty

Deducting Present vs. Past Events

100% Sure (Positive Deduction)

must be / must have + V3

"The lock wasn't broken; the intruder must have had a key."

50-70% Possible (Uncertain Hypothesis)

might / could / may / could have + V3

"The strange light might have been a drone or satellite."

100% Impossible (Negative Deduction)

can't be / couldn't have + V3

"He can't have stolen the artifact; he was on camera in London."

Pillar 2

Obligation & Need

Mandates & Bureau Protocols

have to / has to / had to (External Rule)

"Detectives have to log all physical evidence immediately."

need to / needed to (Necessity)

"We need to analyze the sound recording for background noise."

must + Infinitive (Direct Bureau Requirement)

"You must preserve the crime scene integrity."

Pillar 3

Advice & Warnings

Investigative Recommendations

should / shouldn't (Good Advice)

"You should double-check the witness testimony."

ought to (Formal Duty Advice)

"We ought to report this anomaly to the Bureau Director."

had better (not) (Urgent Warning / Risk)

"We had better secure the perimeter before dark!"

Pillar 4

Opinion Starters

Structuring Detective Debriefs

In my opinion...

"In my opinion, the footprints were left intentionally to mislead us."

I suspect that...

"I suspect that the suspect had insider access."

It seems to me that... / As far as I'm concerned...

"It seems to me that time was altered inside the cavern."
