Describing Past Habits: Used To, Would & Simple Past

To discuss past habits, routines, and states that are no longer true, English uses three distinct structures depending on whether you are describing an action habit, a past state, or a single finished event.

Grammar Rules Comparison Matrix

Structure Action Verbs (run, play, bake) State Verbs (be, live, have, like) Single Past Events (2018, yesterday)
used to + verb ✅ Allowed ("used to play") ✅ Allowed ("used to live") ❌ NO! (Habits only)
would + verb ✅ Allowed ("would play") ❌ FORBIDDEN! (*would live*) ❌ NO! (Routines only)
Simple Past (V2) ✅ Allowed ("played") ✅ Allowed ("lived") ✅ REQUIRED ("moved in 2021")
Structure 1

used to + base verb

Used for repeated past actions AND past states that are no longer true.

Action Verb:

"I used to play tennis every weekend."

State Verb:

"I used to live in Madrid."

Negative Form:

"I didn't use to like spinach." (Drop the 'd' after didn't!)

Structure 2

would + base verb

Used for repeated past ACTIONS to add nostalgic tone. Never with states!

Repeated Action Routine:

"Every summer, we would visit our grandparents."

CRITICAL B1 RULE:

NEVER use 'would' with State Verbs!

❌ "I would live in London."
✅ "I used to live in London."

Structure 3

Simple Past V2

Required for single completed events, specific dates, or fixed durations.

Single Finished Event:

"In 2018, I moved to Canada."

Fixed Time Duration:

"I lived in Paris for 3 years."

Contrast Rule:

Cannot use 'used to' or 'would' for 1-time occurrences!
