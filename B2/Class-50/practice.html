Section 2 • Grammar Focus

Reduced Relative Clauses

Learn how to make sentences more concise and natural by reducing defining relative clauses.

Grammar Reference

Three Ways to Reduce a Relative Clause

Click any highlighted clause pill below to toggle between Full and Reduced forms!

1 Drop pronoun + be

You can shorten a relative clause by dropping the relative pronoun (who / that) and the verb be.

Someone to think quickly might be a good surgeon.
A person for adventure could be a private detective.
A person in music might be a good DJ.
2 Drop pronoun + use gerund

You can also drop who / that and change an active verb to the gerund (-ing).

Someone job security might not want to be a jazz musician.
Tip: Works even when the original verb is in the simple present (needs → needing, hopes → hoping).
3 Replace have with “with”

In many relative clauses, who / that + the verb have can be replaced by with.

A person a good voice could be a good TV journalist.
Tip: Use with for physical traits, possessions, or qualities (who has patience → with patience).
