Grammar Core

Past Negative Questions & Tag Questions

Learn how to offer opinions about past events, invite reactions, and handle special tag question pronouns (they, affirmative tags for negative subjects like nobody / nothing).

Rule 1

Past Negative Questions

Express surprise or check if the listener agrees with your opinion about past events.

  • Wasn't it weird how the manager's microphone kept turning on and off?
  • Didn't it seem like the manager's speech would never end?
  • Shouldn't the company have provided us with coffee?
Rule 2

Past Tag Questions

Affirmative statement + Negative tag (or vice versa).

  • The manager's speech was really boring, wasn't it?
  • The manager has given some boring speeches, hasn't he?
  • The speech had put everyone to sleep, hadn't it?
Rule 3

Indefinite Pronouns use "THEY"

When subject is somebody, someone, everybody, everyone, nobody, no one.

Everyone had a cell phone in school, didn't they?

✓ Answer: Yes, they did.

Somebody has hacked into your computer, haven't they?

✓ Answer: Yes, they have. / No, they haven't.

Rule 4

Negative Subjects (Nobody / Nothing)

Negative subjects make the main clause negative, requiring an AFFIRMATIVE tag.

Nobody left any voice mail messages, did they?

✓ Answer: Yes, they did. / No, they didn't.

There was nothing he could do with his computer, was there?

✓ Answer: No, there wasn't.
