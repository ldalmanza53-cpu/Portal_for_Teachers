B1/B2 Paragraph Architecture

Crafting Sophisticated Cultural Comparison Paragraphs

Strong academic and B1/B2 level writing goes beyond simple facts. Learn how to combine a Topic Sentence with Contrast Support, Exception Support, and a sentence-modifying Consequence Clause (, which).

4-Stage Structural Flowchart

Click cards to inspect details
Stage 1

Topic Sentence

State the general cultural habit, custom, or social norm being examined.

Stage 2

Contrast Support

Highlight how another culture or system differs using linkers like whereas, while, unlike.

Stage 3

Exception Support

Refine your statement by noting exceptions (*except for, apart from*).

Stage 4

Consequence (, which)

Comment on the whole situation using a sentence-modifying relative clause.

Grammar Deck: Contrast Linkers
whereas / while + Clause [S + V]

Used to connect two contrasting clauses.

"She prefers tea, whereas he loves espresso."

unlike / in contrast to + Noun Phrase / -ing

Prepositions directly followed by a noun or noun phrase.

"Unlike fast-paced city dwellers, small town residents take slow lunches."

on the other hand Transition (Starts sentence)

Requires a full stop before it and a comma right after.

"Some cultures prioritize brevity. On the other hand, others value warm greetings."

Grammar Deck: Exception Phrases
except for / apart from + Noun Phrase

Identifies an exclusion from a general statement.

"Except for major public holidays, shops remain open every day."

with the exception of + Formal Noun Phrase

Formal academic phrase used to exclude items.

"With the exception of small family bakeries, businesses close at 8 PM."

except when + Clause [S + V]

Used when the exception is a full conditional clause.

"People shake hands, except when they are greeting close personal friends."

2. Sentence Combination Exercises

Combine each pair of clauses using the indicated linker or exception phrase in brackets.

1. Northern cities prioritize cars. Local authorities here close main avenues on Sunday. [whereas]

2. Most shops lock their doors for afternoon rest. Supermarkets stay open all day. [except for]

3. Fast coffee runs dominate overseas. Local residents savor slow morning brews. [, which allows]
