Grammar Plus (Ex 16) Reference Guide

Noun Clauses After be

Complex introductory phrases ending with prepositions are followed by a gerund phrase, not + gerund phrase, or a noun phrase, followed by is (that) and a noun clause.

...about + Gerund

The hard part about being a twin is (that) people call you the wrong name.

...of + Gerund

The downside of sharing a bedroom is (that) it's hard to have privacy.

...to + Gerund

The trick to living in a crowded house is (that) you need private space.

...with + Gerund

One difficulty with being the youngest is (that) everyone tells you what to do.

Exercise 17 • Vocabulary Plus

Compound Family Terms Wedding Conversation

Combine prefixes (great-, grand-, great-grand-) and suffix (-in-law) with base family words.

Sara: Hi, Alex! The whole family is here at the wedding! Do you see my (1) great-grandmother over there?
Alex: Is she your mom's grandmother or your dad's grandmother?
Sara: My mom's. And look, there's Raul. He's married to Hugo's older sister. He's Hugo's , but they get along better than most brothers.
Alex: Who's your favorite relative?
Sara: Oh, my Vera, my grandmother's sister. Although Vera has five besides me, I'm probably the closest. She lived next door when I was growing up and taught me to play the guitar.
Alex: That's cool...
Sara: Hey, do you see my Pat making my dad laugh? Pat is my father's uncle. It's always fun to hear his stories!
Alex: So, who is the other person with them?
Sara: Oh, that's my , you know, my dad's grandfather. Let's go and say hello.
