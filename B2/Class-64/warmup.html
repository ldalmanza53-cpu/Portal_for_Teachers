3 Core Irritation Grammar Patterns

Master how native speakers construct rants using frequency adverbs, dummy subjects, and cleft focus structures.

Structure A

Present Continuous + Always / Constantly

Expresses habitual, repeated behavior that causes ongoing annoyance.

Formula:
[Subject] + [is/are] + [always/constantly] + [Verb-ing]
"He is always interrupting me during Zoom meetings!"
Structure B

Introductory Dummy Subject "It"

Shifts the focus directly onto the emotional reaction before introducing the annoying trigger.

Formula:
It + [drives me crazy / bugs me] + [when / if ...]
"It drives me up the wall when people talk loudly on speakerphone."
Structure C

Cleft Sentences ("What ... is ...")

Creates rhetorical emphasis by isolating the specific element that causes peak irritation.

Formula:
What + [bugs me most] + is + [that / when ...]
"What gets on my nerves most is when people leave dirty dishes in the sink."
