Understanding Topic Sentences vs. Thesis Statements

A topic sentence states the main idea of a single paragraph. A thesis statement introduces the main topic and unifying purpose of the whole composition.

Key Functions of a Thesis Statement
  • Contains a single clear idea.
  • Is neither too general nor too specific.
  • Unifies all paragraphs in the composition.
  • Is typically located at the beginning or end of the first paragraph.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Too General: "I've made some bad decisions." (Lacks depth or focus).
  • Too Specific: "I won $500 on a lottery ticket." (Only covers one detail).
  • Off-Topic: Focuses on a sub-detail rather than the central lesson.

Sample Composition: Learning from Decisions

[Paragraph 1] When faced with a decision that tests my values, I reflect on what past situations have taught me. In my life, I have experienced both positive choices and regrets. [??? Thesis Statement Location ???]

[Paragraph 2] One of my positive decisions brought me closer to a friend. A classmate bought me a birthday lottery ticket and jokingly asked to split any prize. When I won $500, I was tempted to keep it all for a new tablet. However, I chose to share half the prize with her. Doing the honest thing strengthened our friendship, and she remains one of my best friends today.

[Paragraph 3] Conversely, a poor decision strained another relationship. I recommended a classmate for a position at my company despite knowing her weak work habits. When she was let go three months later due to poor performance, my employer questioned my professional judgment. That experience taught me the cost of making dishonest recommendations.

[Paragraph 4] Ultimately, every decision—whether good or bad—contributes to our personal growth. By reflecting on our choices, even our past mistakes prepare us to make wiser decisions in the future.
