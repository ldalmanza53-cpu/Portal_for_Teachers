📖 Pragmatic Topic Framing Reference Handbook

1. Essential Topic Framing Patterns

Avoid repetitive verbs like "said" or "talked about". Frame topics using anchor phrases:

  • Their conversation revolved around + [Noun / Gerund]: "Their conversation revolved around finding affordable housing."
  • They centered their discussion on + [Noun Phrase]: "They centered their discussion on Q4 budget reallocations."
  • They touched upon [Topic A], but mostly focused on [Topic B]: "They touched upon weekend plans, but mostly focused on Monday's presentation."

2. Tone Categorization Matrix

💬 Casual / Social

reminiscing about, shooting the breeze, catching up on, bantering about.

💼 Professional / Task

talking shop, hashing out, aligning on, deliberating over.

⚔️ Debate / Conflict

bickering over, clashing over, wrangling over, arguing regarding.

❤️ Emotional / Support

commiserating over, venting about, airing grievances regarding.

3. Preposition & Noun Clause Collocations

Pair verbs with their precise preposition or embedded wh- clause:

• Bickering / Clashing + over / about

• Conferring / Aligning + on / regarding

• Speculating + about / on

• Debating + whether to / whether...

• Discussing + how to / why / what... (Note: Never "discussing about")
