Infinitive and Gerund Phrases in Social English

Master how to express cultural etiquette, social expectations, and personal feelings using Infinitive phrases (It's + adj + to verb) and Gerund phrases (Verb-ing + is + adj).

1. Infinitive & Gerund Equivalence

Rule 1

In sentences with It's + adjective + infinitive, you can follow the adjective with for + object (pronoun or noun).

Infinitive Structure with Object:

"It's difficult for her to talk about her feelings openly."

"It's customary for North Americans to make frequent eye contact."

Equivalent Gerund Subject:

"Talking about her feelings openly is difficult for her."

"Making frequent eye contact is customary for North Americans."

2. Negative Forms: not Placement

Rule 2

For sentences in the negative, place not directly before the infinitive (not + infinitive) or before the gerund (not + gerund).

Negative Infinitive:

"It's considered rude not to thank people who give you gifts."

Negative Gerund:

"Not thanking people who give you gifts is considered rude."

3. Adjectives of Feeling Exception

Rule 3

Adjectives of feeling (glad, happy, pleased, delighted, surprised) CANNOT be used with the dummy "It's + adjective + infinitive" structure! Instead, the sentence must state WHO has the feelings: Subject + be + adjective + infinitive.

INCORRECT:

"It's happy for parents to see their children go to college."

CORRECT FORM:

"Most parents are happy to see their children go to college."

"People are always delighted to get compliments."

Grammar Decision Logic Guide

Is the main adjective a Feeling?

(e.g., happy, glad, pleased, surprised, delighted)

→ Use: Subject + be + adj + infinitive
("Tom is always happy to lend...")

Is it a general evaluation adjective?

(e.g., unusual, relaxing, inappropriate, polite, customary)

→ Use: It's + adj + (for + person) + infinitive
("It's unusual for Wendy to arrive...")
