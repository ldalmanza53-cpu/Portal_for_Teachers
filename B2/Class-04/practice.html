Grammar Reference • Textbook Page Class 3

Noun Clauses After Be

A noun clause is a part of a sentence with a subject and a predicate that functions as a noun. That is optional after be.

Evaluative Noun The nice thing / The trouble / An advantage
+
Preposition about / with / of
+
Gerund Phrase being a twin / having a sister
+
Verb Be + (That) is (that)
+
Noun Clause Predicate I get a lot of attention.
Textbook Example 1

"The only trouble with being a two-income family is (that) we don't spend as much time together."

Preposition: with | Gerund: being | Noun clause: (that) we don't spend...

Textbook Example 2

"The big advantage of having Grandma at home is (that) she can babysit more often."

Preposition: of | Gerund: having | Noun clause: (that) she can babysit...
