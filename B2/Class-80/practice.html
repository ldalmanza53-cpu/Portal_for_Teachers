Future Points of Reference with Future Aspects

When using the Future Perfect or Future Perfect Continuous, a specific point in the future is introduced by a time expression or another clause in the sentence.

Future Perfect (Completed Action)

Emphasizes that an action will be completed prior to the future reference point:

🔹 By this time next year, your commitment to language study is going to have gotten stronger.

🔹 Before next spring, he will have finished most of his course work.

🔹 By the time you arrive in New York, Marisa will have already received the package.

Future Perfect Continuous (Duration)

Emphasizes the duration of an ongoing action up to that future reference point:

🔹 On August 1, I will have been living overseas for six months.

🔹 When the van arrives, I will have been packing for two days.
