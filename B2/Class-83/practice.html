Mixed Conditionals Guide

Conditionals can appear in many forms. They describe how situations in the past affect situations in the past, the present, or the future.

1. True Events in the Past Past → Past

Use a past form in both the if-clause and the result clause to talk about true events/habits in the past.

• "When I was younger, if I didn't behave well, my parents were disappointed."

• "If we got lost during our trip last year, we just asked someone for directions."

2. Hypothetical Past → Recent Past Past → Past

Use past perfect in the if-clause and would/wouldn't have + past participle in the result clause.

• "If I had been born with a good voice, I would have started my own band a long time ago."

3. Hypothetical Past → Present Past → Now

Use past perfect in the if-clause and would/wouldn't + base verb in the result clause to describe past situations with effects on today.

• "If I had studied harder when I was in school, I would have a better job today."

4. Hypothetical Past → Future Past → Future

Use past perfect in the if-clause and would/wouldn't + verb (or be + -ing) in the result clause for future effects.

• "If she had booked her flight before now, she would be in Paris next week."

• "If I hadn't taken a year off from school, I would be graduating this June."
