Present Perfect vs. Past & Continuous Rules

Master when to use Simple Past (completed events with specific time clues), Present Perfect Simple (repeated events, results, stative verbs, completed news actions), and Present Perfect Continuous (ongoing actions with verbs like live, work, study, teach).

Simple Past vs. Present Perfect

Time Specificity
1. Repeated Event (May Continue into Present):

"Thieves have robbed three banks this year."

"The seal has painted four pictures so far."

2. Present Effect / Present Relevance:

"She's been more careful since she lost her car keys."

"The store has had a security camera for a month now."

⚡ Key Rule: Specific finished past time (yesterday, last year, when he sold...) requires Simple Past. Unspecified time up to now (since, so far, this year, yet, already) requires Present Perfect.

Present Perfect vs. Continuous

Ongoing Verbs
Ongoing Action Verbs (Both Tenses Allowed):

• Verbs like live, work, study, teach, take lessons express ongoing action.

• "He has lived in London for 8 years." = "He has been living in London for 8 years."

• "I have taken lessons..." = "I have been taking lessons..."

⚠️ Stative / Result / Completed Actions (Simple ONLY):

• Stative verbs (be, possess, know, love) or completed single decisions (decide, reach 36°C, already put up) CANNOT be continuous!

• Correct: "The pier has been in bad condition." (Not has been being)
