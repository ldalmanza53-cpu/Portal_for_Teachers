Unit 9 Grammar Guide

Clauses vs. Phrases: Contrast & Exception

Master how to express contrasting ideas and exceptions using clauses (Subject + Verb) and phrases (Noun / Pronoun).

1. Showing Contrast

Comparing two opposing facts or opinions

Introduces a CLAUSE (Subject + Verb) whereas / while

• Whereas the bottled water market is huge in Italy, it is very small in Japan.
• While Leo prefers a big breakfast, I just have coffee.

Introduces a PHRASE (Noun / Pronoun) unlike / in contrast to

• Unlike me, all my friends are addicted to reality shows on TV.
• In contrast to city people, those who live on farms must have a car.

2. Showing Exceptions

Pointing out something that differs from the rest of a group

Exception with a NOUN / PHRASE except (for) / with the exception of

• Everyone in my family, except for my mother, plays a musical instrument.
• Everyone on our street, with the exception of my family, has a pet.

Exception with a CLAUSE except that / except for the fact that

• I'm a typical Canadian, except for the fact that I don't like hockey.
• They are very different, except that they are both in their 20s.
