Describing Typical Behaviors, Contrasts & Exceptions

To describe human character and habits with B1/B2 accuracy, combine Expressions of Habit (tend to, is inclined to), Contrast Markers (whereas, unlike), and Exception Structures (except for, except when).

Pillar 1: Habits

Expressions of Habit

Used to state people's natural tendencies and routine social traits.

tend to + Verb Base Infinitive

"She tends to work late when inspired."

have a habit of + -ing Gerund

"He has a habit of interrupting others."

is inclined to + Verb Tendency

"She is inclined to analyze every detail."

As a rule, [Clause] General Principle

"As a rule, introverts prefer small groups."

Pillar 2: Contrasts

Contrast Markers

Highlight differences between two individuals or opposing habits.

whereas / while + Full Clause

"He plans ahead, whereas I am spontaneous."

unlike / in contrast to + Noun Phrase

"Unlike my brother, I enjoy public speaking."

On the other hand, Sentence Transition

"She stays calm. On the other hand, he panics."

Pillar 3: Exceptions

Exception Phrases

Refine general statements by stating conditions or exclusions.

except for / apart from + Noun / -ing

"He eats everything, apart from mushrooms."

with the exception of Formal Noun Phrase

"With the exception of Mondays, she runs daily."

except when + Full Clause

"She sleeps early, except when exams approach."
