Explaining Past Habits: Used To vs Would

To describe life in the past, English speakers combine used to (habits & states), would (nostalgic repeated actions only), and Simple Past (single finished events).

Structure 1

used to + verb

Used for past habits AND past states that are no longer true today.

Action Verb Example:

"I used to play tennis every weekend."

State Verb Example:

"I used to live in Madrid."

Negative Trap:

"I didn't use to like spinach." (Note: 'use', not 'used'!)

Structure 2

would + verb

Used for repeated past ACTIONS. Expresses nostalgia and storytelling routines.

Repeated Action:

"Every summer, we would visit my grandparents."

CRITICAL B1 RULE:

NEVER use 'would' with State Verbs!

❌ "I would live in London."
✅ "I used to live in London."

Structure 3

Simple Past V2

Required for single completed events, specific time points, or duration stating.

Single Past Event:

"In 2018, I moved to Canada."

Fixed Time Duration:

"I lived in Paris for 3 years."

Contrast Rule:

Cannot use 'used to' or 'would' for single 1-time actions!

1. Grammar Summary Reference

USED TO + Verb

• Action habits AND state verbs

• Negative: didn't use to

WOULD + Verb

• Repeated actions ONLY

• ❌ Never with state verbs (*live, have, be*)

SIMPLE PAST

• Finished 1-time past events

• Specific dates / durations
