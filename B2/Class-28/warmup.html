Digital Wellbeing & Language Handbook

Reference guide for the 4-step Habit Loop, key digital terms, and advice structures.

1. The 4-Step Habit Loop (Behavioral Science)

  • 1. Cue (Make it Invisible): The trigger that initiates the behavior (e.g., app notification). Remove cues to eliminate habits.
  • 2. Craving (Make it Unattractive): The motivational force behind the habit. Change your mindset regarding tech compulsions.
  • 3. Response (Make it Difficult): The actual action. Increase friction (e.g., log out, put phone in another room).
  • 4. Reward (Make it Satisfying): The benefit gained. Substitute bad tech rewards with positive offline rewards.

2. Digital Misuse Vocabulary

  • Doomscrolling: Obsessively scrolling through negative social media news feeds.
  • Phubbing (Phone + Snubbing): Snubbing or ignoring companions in social settings by looking at your phone.
  • Continuous Partial Attention: Rapidly switching attention between screens and tasks without deep focus.
  • Screen Fatigue: Eye strain, headache, and cognitive exhaustion caused by prolonged screen time.

3. Advice & Habit Target Grammar

  • Had better + V1 (base verb): Strong advice or warning. ("You had better turn off notifications.")
  • Ought to + V1: Moral obligation or good recommendation. ("We ought to spend more time outdoors.")
  • Used to + V1 vs Get used to + -ing: Past habit vs adapting to a new routine. ("I used to check social media at 2 AM, but I am getting used to leaving my phone in the kitchen.")
  • In order to / So as to + V1: Purpose. ("I put my phone on grayscale in order to reduce cravings.")
  • Unless + Positive verb: Except if. ("Unless you set strict timers, social media will consume your evening.")
