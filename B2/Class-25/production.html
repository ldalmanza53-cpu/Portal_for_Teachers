Sleep Idioms & Expressions Bank

Essential 12 B1 level idioms for sleep hygiene & circadian routines

1. hit the hay / hit the sack

Meaning: To go to bed to sleep.

"After a long workday, I usually hit the hay at 10 PM."

2. sleep like a log

Meaning: To sleep very deeply and soundly.

"If I avoid coffee after 2 PM, I sleep like a log all night."

3. toss and turn

Meaning: To move around uncomfortably in bed trying to sleep.

"When I use my phone in bed, I toss and turn for hours."

4. nod off

Meaning: To fall asleep unintentionally for a short time.

"He was so tired that he began to nod off during the lecture."

5. light sleeper

Meaning: A person who wakes up very easily at small noises.

"My roommate is a light sleeper, so I wear soft slippers."

6. heavy sleeper

Meaning: A person who sleeps deeply and isn't easily woken.

"She is a heavy sleeper and needs three loud alarms."

7. early bird

Meaning: Someone who wakes up very early and works best in the morning.

"An early bird gets the most productive work done at dawn."

8. night owl

Meaning: Someone who stays up late and feels active at night.

"Alex is a night owl, but he suffers from morning fatigue."

9. pull an all-nighter

Meaning: To stay awake all night studying or working.

"Pulling an all-nighter wrecks your circadian focus."

10. catch some Zs

Meaning: Informal expression meaning to get some sleep.

"I'm exhausted, so I'm going to catch some Zs before dinner."

11. sleep in

Meaning: To sleep later than usual in the morning.

"On weekends, I usually sleep in until 9:00 AM."

12. wide awake

Meaning: Completely awake and fully alert.

"After taking a cold morning shower, I was wide awake."
