B1 Common Pitfalls Matrix & Diagnostic Quiz

Avoid false friends, relative pronoun errors, and harsh unsoftened statements!

1. Sympathetic vs. Friendly / Nice

False Friend Trap

In English, 'sympathetic' means showing understanding or compassion for someone in distress (compasivo). It does NOT mean "simpático". For "simpático", use friendly, nice, or pleasant.

❌ "He is very sympathetic, everyone likes him."
✅ "He is very friendly and easy to talk to."

2. Sensitive vs. Sensible

Confusion Alert

Sensitive = easily hurt or emotionally attentive (sensible).
Sensible = practical, wise, and showing good judgment (sensato/juicioso).

❌ "She made a sensitive choice for the budget."
✅ "She made a sensible choice for the budget."

3. People Pronouns: Who vs. Which

Relative Clause Rule

When describing people or classmates, always use who or that. Never use which for humans!

❌ "She is a student which helps everyone."
✅ "She is a student who helps everyone."

4. Softening Negative Traits

Polite Pragmatics

Calling someone blunt adjectives like "stubborn" or "impulsive" directly sounds rude. Use softeners: a bit, tend to be, fairly, at times.

❌ "Leo is stubborn." (Too harsh)
✅ "Leo can be a bit stubborn at times."
