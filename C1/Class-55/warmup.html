Comparison & Attitude Matrix

Upgrade simple A2 statements into sophisticated B1+ comparative sentences using relative clauses and contrast connectors.

1

Defining Relative Clauses

[Subject] + is someone/a quality + who/that + [Verb...]

Identifies or defines essential communicator traits and speaker behaviors.

"An articulate presenter is someone who communicates ideas with clarity."

2

Contrast Connectors

While/Whereas [Clause A], [Clause B]

Contrasts opposing attitudes like glossophobia vs exhilaration.

"While reluctant speakers view speeches as a threat, confident presenters embrace them."

3

Comparative Evaluations

In contrast to / Compared with [Noun], [Subject] [Verb]

Evaluates differences between perfectionism and connection.

"In contrast to perfectionist presenters, empathetic speakers prioritize connection."

🚀A2 Basic Statements vs B1+ Upgraded Structures

Observe how adding relative clauses and contrast connectors builds communicative fluency.

BASIC (CEFR A2) Simple Contrast

"Good speakers talk clearly. Poor speakers are nervous."

UPGRADED (CEFR B1+) Relative Clause + Whereas

"An effective communicator is someone who speaks articulately, whereas anxious presenters often stumble over words."

BASIC (CEFR A2) Simple Contrast

"Some people hate public speaking. Others like it."

UPGRADED (CEFR B1+) While-Clause Contrast

"While reluctant individuals view public speaking as a threat, confident presenters regard it as a growth opportunity."

BASIC (CEFR A2) Simple Contrast

"Nervous speakers worry about errors. Good speakers focus on message."

UPGRADED (CEFR B1+) Unlike + Relative Clause

"Unlike perfectionist speakers who fear making mistakes, empathetic communicators prioritize audience connection."

BASIC (CEFR A2) Simple Contrast

"Active listening is good. It helps understand people."

UPGRADED (CEFR B1+) Trait Definition + Compared with

"Active listening is a vital trait that allows a speaker to respond thoughtfully, compared with presenters who ignore audience cues."
