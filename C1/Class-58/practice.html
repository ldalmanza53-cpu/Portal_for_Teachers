Subject-Verb Agreement with Quantifiers

Quantifiers modify singular, plural, and uncountable nouns. Learn when to use singular vs. plural verbs!

1. Uncountable vs. Plural

All (of), a lot of, lots of, plenty of, some (of), most (of), and fractions take a singular verb with uncountable nouns, and a plural verb with plural nouns.

Uncountable: A lot of grammar is complicated.
Plural: Most people don't need to write well.
Fraction: Three-quarters of emails contain errors.

2. Collective & Partitive

Each of, every one of, none of, and collectives like majority (of), minority (of) typically take a singular verb, but often take plural verbs in informal speech.

Formal: None of us has the right.
Informal: None of us have the right.
Collective: The majority of teens use / uses slang.

3. Always Singular

Everyone, someone, anyone, no one, each + N, every + N are ALWAYS followed by a singular verb.

Every + N: Every variety is correct.
No one: No one expects email to be correct.
