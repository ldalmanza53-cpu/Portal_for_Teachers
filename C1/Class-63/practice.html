Section 4: Rules & Patterns Guide

Review structural rules, hyphenation conventions, and comparative forms for B1 compound adjectives.

Many compound adjectives are formed by combining a noun with a past participle (-ed / irregular third form):

awestruck frostbitten handwritten homemade store-bought waterlogged bloodstained heartbroken moth-eaten sunburned windswept handmade

Many standard compound adjectives have become so frequently used in English that modern dictionaries write them as a single word without hyphens:

airborne • barefooted • downhearted • lightweight • painstaking • airsick • daylong • hardheaded • newfound • seaworthy
Important Rule: In the comparative form of compound adjectives, more and less are NOT followed by hyphens!
  • a more forward-looking plan (NO hyphen after "more")
  • a less easygoing person (NO hyphen after "less")
  • a more highly trained applicant
