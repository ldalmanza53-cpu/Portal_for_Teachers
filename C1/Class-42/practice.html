Subject-Verb Inversion with as in Comparisons

When as introduces a clause expressing a comparison, subject-verb inversion can occur in affirmative sentences.

With do / did / does:

"Marissa has trouble accepting change, as does Trina."

With Auxiliary Verbs:

"Mitt has coped well, as have his co-workers."

With Modals:

"Grandma would tell us stories, as would Grandpa."

With be (is / are):

"Marcel is wary of tech, as is his whole family."

Shortened & Lengthened as if / as though Clauses

Shortening Clauses (Removing subject + 'be'):

When both clauses have the same subject, as if and as though clauses with adjectives or past participles are frequently shortened:

"Bill is talking about quitting his job, as though (he were) single and carefree."

Unreal / Hypothetical Past Tense:

Use a past form of a verb after as if / as though when followed by a hypothetical or unreal situation:

"Some young people replace their gadgets every year as if they had all the money in the world."
