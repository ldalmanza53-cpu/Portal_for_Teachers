DNA for Information Storage

Incredibly, scientists can now synthesize DNA to hold digital information, such as a video or text. The information can later be read by machines called DNA sequencers. DNA offers the longest duration for digital storage, keeping data safe for tens of thousands of years.

Benefit: Infinite storage density without needing energy to maintain data.

Danger: Extremely expensive technology; potential privacy or bio-security risks.

Thought Identification

Technology can already identify thoughts from scans of activity patterns in the human brain. The first attempts identified simple thoughts, but researchers are finding ways to reveal more complex thoughts and intentions.

Benefit: Allows paralyzed individuals or non-verbal patients to communicate freely.

Danger: Loss of mind privacy, potential misuse by invasive advertising or governments.

Animal Cloning

With just one cell from a live or dead animal, an exact copy of the animal can be created with a technique known as cloning. The technique has been used to clone various pets, and cloned horses can even take part in the Olympics now.

Benefit: Preservation of endangered species; medical research advancements.

Danger: Reduced genetic diversity; ethical concerns regarding animal health.

Indefinite & Definite Articles Rules

Master when to use 'a/an', 'the', or 'Ø' (no article / zero article). Review the 4 primary rules extracted from textbook examples.

1

First Mention vs. Second Mention

Use an indefinite article (a / an) when mentioning a singular countable noun for the first time. Use the when referring to the same item again. Use Ø (no article) for plural/uncountable nouns on first mention.

"With just one cell from a live or dead animal, an exact copy of the animal can be created."

2

General Statements (Plural vs Singular)

If you use a plural noun to make a general statement, do NOT use an article (Ø). If you make the same statement with a singular noun, use the or a / an.

[Ø] "Technology can identify thoughts in human brains." (Plural general)

[the / a] "Technology can identify thoughts in the / a human brain." (Singular general)

3

Uncountable Nouns in General Statements

When making a general statement, do NOT use an article (Ø) with uncountable nouns such as technology, education, shopping, love, storage, DNA.

"DNA offers the longest duration for digital storage." (No articles before DNA or storage)

4

Superlatives, Sequence Markers & Time Expressions

Use the with superlatives (the most interesting) and sequence markers (the first, the last, the next). BUT do NOT use the with time expressions like last night, next month, next year.

"The first attempts identified simple thoughts." (Sequence)

"I'm sure a new computer will be cheaper next year." (Time expression - No 'the')
