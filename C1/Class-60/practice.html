Grammar Plus • Page T-146 Sec. 15

Subject-Verb Agreement with Quantifiers

Learn how verbs agree with expressions of quantity, collective nouns like majority or minority, and singular pronouns.

1

A number of + Plural Noun

A (large / small / great) number of always modifies a plural noun and takes a plural verb.

"A large number of students in my English class were absent on Friday."
2

Majority / Minority as Single Unit

When collective nouns like majority or minority act as a single group or unit, they take a singular verb.

"All students can express their opinions, but the majority rules."

"Spanish speakers constitute a linguistic minority that is growing rapidly."

3

Majority / Minority + Plural Complement

Majority and minority are followed by the plural verb form when the complement refers to individual members of a plural noun. In informal speech, both singular & plural verbs are frequently acceptable!

"The majority are slackers." / "Only a small minority are linguists."

4

Always Singular Pronouns

Words like Each, every, everyone, someone, anyone, no one, each + noun, every + noun are ALWAYS followed by a singular verb.

"Every variety is correct."

"No one expects email to be correct."
