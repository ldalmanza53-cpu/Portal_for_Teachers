Overcoming a Bad First Impression

Interactive Reading Article (Susan Fee)

Hover over or click highlighted terms for B1 vocabulary explanations.

Have any of these situations happened to you? Forgetting someone’s name after you’ve just met, spilling coffee on your potential boss during an interview, or unintentionally insulting a co-worker on your first day? Ouch! You never have a second chance to make a first impression, so what happens when that first impression is negative? Here is how you can recover.

1. Apologize Immediately

As soon as you realize you may have offended someone, address it. The more time that passes, the more the story can become blown out of proportion.

2. Avoid Over-Apologizing

Saying you're sorry is important, but overdoing it creates another uncomfortable situation. If you repeatedly bring up the past, groveling and begging, you defeat your purpose.

3. Make No Assumptions

Don't start with "You must think I'm a total idiot." Say something like: "I'm uncomfortable with how I behaved yesterday..."

4. Be Sincere (3 Steps)

First, don't blame external factors. Second, acknowledge how your actions affected the person. Third, explain what you will do differently in the future.

Cleft Sentences with What & Cleft Expressions

Master cleft sentence structures used to add emphasis in English. Practice converting standard sentences into 'What... is/was' and 'the reason why', 'the place where', etc.

Grammar Rule Overview

1. Cleft Sentences with What

To emphasize the whole sentence or verb action rather than just the object, use a cleft sentence with what and a form of the verb do:

STANDARD: I try to project a positive attitude.
EMPHASIZED CLEFT: What I do is try to project a positive attitude.

2. Cleft Expressions

Include expressions like the reason why, the thing that, the place where, the day when, and the person who + verb be:

STANDARD: I'm wearing sunglasses to protect my eyes.
EMPHASIZED CLEFT: The reason why I'm wearing sunglasses is to protect my eyes.
