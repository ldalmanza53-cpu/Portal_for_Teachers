Quick Rule for Optional Pronouns:

When the relative pronoun is the object of the clause (followed by a noun/pronoun + verb), it is OPTIONAL. When it is the subject or shows possession (whose), it is REQUIRED!

Defining Relative Clauses Master Reference

Essential B1 Grammar Matrix based on ESL textbook standards.

Grammar Function Relative Pronouns Is Pronoun Required? Example Sentence B1 Rule / Tip
Subject of Clause (S) who / that / which REQUIRED "People who are concerned about pesticides buy organic food." Followed immediately by a VERB (are). Cannot omit!
Object of Clause (O) that / which / who / whom OPTIONAL "Hybrid cars are an option (that) more people are choosing." Followed by a SUBJECT + VERB (more people are choosing). Pronoun can be dropped!
Showing Possession (P) whose REQUIRED "Professionals whose managers allow it work from home." Replaces possessive adjectives (their managers -> whose managers).

The B1 "Golden Test" for Omission

Read the words right after the relative pronoun:

  • Pronoun + Verb = REQUIRED (e.g., "that offer workouts")
  • Pronoun + Noun / Pronoun = OPTIONAL (e.g., "that people choose")

Who vs. Whom vs. That

In modern spoken English, who or that are commonly used for people in object clauses. Whom is formal and restricted mostly to writing or formal contexts (e.g., "children whom they once considered...").
