Reduced Relative Clauses

Shorten relative clauses (with who, which, or that) to make your writing and speech more concise and fluent!

1

Rule 1: Active Verbs

Change verb to present participle (-ing)

Active Voice

Drop the relative pronoun (who / that) and change the active verb into its -ing participle form.

Full Relative Clause:

Someone who hopes to be a chef should get proper training.

Reduced Relative Clause:

Someone hoping to be a chef should get proper training.

2

Rule 2: 'Be' + Adjective / Preposition

Drop relative pronoun + 'be' verb (who is / are)

State / Preposition

When the clause contains be + an adjective phrase or prepositional phrase, omit both who/that and is/are completely!

Full Relative Clause:

Someone who is interested in music trends might be a good DJ.

Reduced Relative Clause:

Someone interested in music trends might be a good DJ.
