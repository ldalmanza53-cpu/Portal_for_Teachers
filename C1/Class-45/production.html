Grammar Framework: Double Object Verbs in Marketing

Direct & Indirect Object Positions

Double object verbs take both a Direct Object (the thing) and an Indirect Object (the recipient person)!

A

Pattern A: Verb + Person + Thing

No Preposition Required

Place the person (Indirect Object) directly after the verb, followed by the item (Direct Object).

"The store offered shoppers a 20% discount."

Person = shoppers | Thing = a 20% discount

B

Pattern B: Verb + Thing + Preposition + Person

Requires 'TO' or 'FOR'

Place the thing (Direct Object) first, then use to (direction/recipient) or for (benefit/creation) before the person.

"The store offered a 20% discount to shoppers."

Use TO (give, send, offer, show, sell) | Use FOR (buy, prepare, make, find)

⚡ Step 2: Sentence Upgrader

Basic B1 vs Upgraded B1+ Marketing Phrases

❌ Basic B1 (Incorrect / Awkward):
"We give discount to shoppers in store."
✔ Upgraded B1+ (Pattern A):
"We offer loyal shoppers a 20% discount at checkout."
❌ Basic B1 (Wrong Preposition):
"Influencers show to followers new products."
✔ Upgraded B1+ (Pattern B):
"Influencers demonstrate product features to their followers."
❌ Basic B1 (Confusing 'to'/'for'):
"Brands buy promotional gifts to customers."
✔ Upgraded B1+ ('For' Benefit Pattern):
"Brands purchase exclusive promotional gifts for VIP clients."
