Noun Clause Structural Matrix

To elevate your English from A2 to B1+, replace simple statements with embedded noun clauses that function as subjects or direct objects of opinion verbs.

1

That-Clause Opinions

[Subject] + [Verb/Adj] + that + [S + V + O]

Expresses firm beliefs, general truths, or logical convictions.

"I am convinced that renewable energy specialists shape our future."

2

Wh- / Whether Interrogative Clauses

Whether/Why/How + [S + V] + [Main Verb]

Evaluates conditions, motives, or complex considerations as full clauses.

"Why youth choose conservation depends on early education."

3

Nominal Relative "What..." Clauses

What + [S + V] + is that + [S + V + O]

Fronts the focal point for strong communicative emphasis.

"What strikes me about marine biology is that it requires patience."

🚀A2 Basic vs B1+ Noun Clause Upgrades

Compare basic simple statements with fluent B1+ embedded noun clause structures.

BASIC (CEFR A2) Simple Statement

"Forest firefighters have a dangerous job. They save trees."

UPGRADED (CEFR B1+) Nominal Relative Clause

"What I admire most about forest firefighters is that they risk their lives to preserve wild habitats."

BASIC (CEFR A2) Simple Statement

"Is wildlife rehabilitation a good career? It depends on passion."

UPGRADED (CEFR B1+) Whether-Clause Subject

"Whether wildlife rehabilitation is suitable for someone depends on their emotional resilience."

BASIC (CEFR A2) Simple Statement

"Marine biologists study oceans. It is very hard work."

UPGRADED (CEFR B1+) Fact-That Clause

"The fact that marine biologists spend months at sea makes this career both challenging and rewarding."

BASIC (CEFR A2) Simple Statement

"Environmental lawyers help nature. I think this is true."

UPGRADED (CEFR B1+) That-Clause Direct Object

"I am convinced that environmental policy advocates play an essential role in passing climate laws."
