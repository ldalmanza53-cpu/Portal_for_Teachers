Grammar Plus • Section 16

Noun Clauses with Whoever & Whomever

Learn when to use the subject form whoever versus the object form whomever in formal written and spoken English.

Whoever (Subject)

Used when the pronoun is the subject of the verb in the noun clause.

"Whoever wants a unique experience should try scuba diving."

Whomever (Object)

Used in formal speech/writing when the pronoun is the object of a verb or preposition.

"I'll take whomever the instructor chooses for my partner."

Whichever (Specific Options)

Used when referring to a known and limited group of items ("whatever one / ones").

"You can choose whichever you want from these three kayaks."
