Sentence Architecture & Formulas

The Contrast & Exception Formula Matrix

Transform basic A2 descriptions like "He likes coffee but she likes tea" into B1+ academic behavior profiles: "He tends to drink espresso every morning, whereas his sister prefers herbal tea, with the exception of cold winter days."

1

Direct Habit Contrast Formula

[Person A] + [tends to / usually] + [Habit A] + [, whereas / , while] + [Person B] + [Habit B]

Contrasts two opposing routines smoothly in a single compound sentence.

✔️ "Leo tends to wake up early, whereas his brother stays in bed until noon."

2

General Rule + Exception Marker

[Generally / Typically,] + [Subject + Routine] + [, with the exception of / except for] + [Exception]

States a regular habit while isolating specific circumstances where it changes.

✔️ "Generally, she walks to work, with the exception of rainy mornings."

3

Conditional Exception Formula

[Subject + rarely / seldom + Routine] + [unless] + [Special Condition]

Expresses an infrequent behavior that only occurs under one unique condition.

✔️ "Marcus rarely eats fast food unless he is traveling for business."

B1+ Quality Criteria Checklist

  • Word count between 80 - 150 words
  • Expresses tendency ("tends to", "inclined to", "habit of")
  • Direct contrast connector ("whereas", "while", "in contrast", "on the other hand")
  • Exception marker used ("except for", "apart from", "with the exception of", "unless")
  • General vs. Occasional contrast ("generally", "occasionally", "rarely")
