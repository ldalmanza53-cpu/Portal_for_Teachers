Reporting Clauses Structure

Use sentences with reporting clauses to report what people say, think, or believe. The word that is optional!

Sentence Structure

[ Subject + Reporting Verb ] + (that) + [ Complete Clause ]

"In Turkey, many people agree (that) when someone goes on a journey..."

"In Venezuela, some people say (that) if someone passes a broom..."

Cultural Spotlight: Great Britain

The Wishing Trees of Great Britain

"In parts of Great Britain, people who aren't feeling well sometimes stick coins into the trunk of a tree that has fallen down. Some people believe that if you put a coin in wood, it can make illnesses go away. They call these trees 'wishing trees.'"

Common Explanations

Listen to people discussing the origins and explanations behind common superstitions.

CD 1 Tracks 16 & 17

Conversations on Allergies, Kidding & Left-Handedness

Dialogue 1 (Sneezing & Evil Spirits): Man explains: "Ancient people believed sneezing allowed evil spirits to enter your body, so saying 'bless you' protects you." The woman with allergies laughs because she sneezed from dust/allergies, not evil spirits.

Dialogue 2 (Mirror Superstition): A son tells a superstitious story to his mother, who becomes deeply worried, forcing the son to clarify he was only joking so she wouldn't stress.

Dialogue 3 (Left-handedness): Man notes: "'Sinister' comes from Latin for 'left', and left-handed people were once viewed suspiciously. But don't mention this to boss Mr. Wilson since he is left-handed!"

Comprehension Questions (Part B)

1. How did the woman with allergies react to the man's explanation?

2. Why did the son feel it was necessary to say that he was only kidding?

3. Why does the man suggest not telling Mr. Wilson that being left-handed was once thought to be suspicious?
