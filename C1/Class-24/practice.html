Adjectives to Describe Truth & Fabrication

Explore how Marla and Chad discuss strange magazine phenomena (upside-down rainbows & glowing mushrooms) using B1 truth/fabrication descriptors.

Describing Likely Truth & Credibility

well-founded

Based on facts, evidence, or sound reasoning.

convincing

Persuasive, cause you to believe it.

plausible

Seemingly reasonable or probable.

credible

Able to be believed; trustworthy.

Describing Fabrication, Doubt & Suspicion

dubious

Not to be relied upon; suspect.

fishy

Arousing feelings of doubt or suspicion.

phony

Not genuine; fake or fraudulent.

far-fetched

Unlikely, improbable, hard to accept.

Complete the Dialogue: Marla & Chad

Select the correct word to complete each speaker's statement.

Marla:
"Why are you reading that silly magazine? You know that most of those stories are at best."
Chad:
"But I enjoy making fun of the articles! Look at this crazy story about a man who saw an upside-down rainbow. Everyone knows that a rainbow's arc is at the top. It sounds pretty to me."
Marla:
"Well, actually, I recently read in a science journal that an upside-down rainbow can occur. There's even a scientific name for it. So I think that story is after all."
Chad:
"Really? Well, OK, here's a story about glowing green mushrooms. It's reported that if you put one on a newspaper in a dark room, it would give off so much light that you could read the words! This story sounds to me. I've never heard of anything like that."
Marla:
"But jellyfish and fireflies give off light, so why do you think it's for mushrooms to glow?"
Chad:
"Well, I haven't seen any evidence that glowing plants exist. But now that you say that, it does make me wonder."

Marla: "Yeah, maybe that 'silly magazine' isn't so silly!"

Sentence Context Explanations:

  1. dubious: Marla considers the magazine silly and suspect/unreliable.
  2. fishy: Chad finds an upside-down rainbow suspicious because he thinks arcs are always at the top.
  3. well-founded: Marla cites a science journal article, proving the phenomenon is scientifically supported.
  4. phony: Chad finds glowing mushrooms fake/unbelievable because he's never heard of them.
  5. far-fetched: Marla compares them to fireflies, questioning why Chad finds glowing fungi improbable.
  6. convincing: Chad admits he hasn't seen persuasive evidence yet.

Reporting Clauses in the Passive with 'It'

Learn how formal and written English uses It + [Passive Verb] + that... to report general beliefs, claims, and news.

Simple Present

It is suggested that...

e.g. It is suggested that passengers report anything suspicious.

Simple Past

It was flatly denied that...

e.g. It was flatly denied that a submarine had been lost.

Present Perfect

It has been estimated that...

e.g. It has been estimated that construction took millions of hours.

Past Perfect

It had been rumored that...

e.g. It had been rumored that a monster lived in the lake.

With Modals

It can't be denied that...

e.g. It couldn't be denied that mysteries elude science.

With Past Modals

It should have been confirmed...

e.g. It should have been confirmed that the flight was delayed.
