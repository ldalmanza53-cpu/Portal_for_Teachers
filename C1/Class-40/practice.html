Career Pivot

Laura

PR Specialist $\rightarrow$ Ski Resort Consultant

"My public-relations job is secure and high-paying. Still, I feel as though something is missing. My real passion is skiing, and today I saw an ad for a PR consultant at a ski resort. It seems like the job was made for me! I'd have to take a big pay cut, but it might be worth it."
Potential Pros:

Pursues true passion, exciting mountain lifestyle, higher job satisfaction.

Potential Cons:

Significant pay cut, loss of corporate security, seasonal work risks.

Education Choice

Ethan

4-Year University vs. Online Certificates

"My parents expect me to go to a four-year college the way they did. I'm not really sure that's for me. I mean, why go away for an education when there are so many online courses? It's as if my computer is a university! For some jobs I'm interested in, I only need a professional certificate, which I could earn online in less than a year."
Potential Pros:

Saves tuition money, faster entry into workforce, flexible schedule, stays home.

Potential Cons:

Parental disagreement, misses campus social life, limited broad degree networking.

Volunteer Life

Maya

Part-time Jobs $\rightarrow$ Volunteer House Builder

"I'm making ends meet thanks to my part-time jobs, but I guess I'm looking for more meaning in my life. I'm thinking of joining a volunteer program to help build houses for the needy as a few of my friends have. I'd get to travel, meet new people, and do something for others."
Potential Pros:

High purpose & fulfillment, travel experiences, building strong friendships.

Potential Cons:

No regular salary, physical hardship, uncertain future career progression.

Grammar Reference • Page 59

As if, as though, as, the way, and like

Learn how to express impressions, comparisons, and informal equivalents in B1 English.

1. Impressions

As if and as though introduce clauses that describe impressions about feelings or behavior after verbs such as act, behave, feel, look, seem, talk.

Examples:

"Still, I feel as if / as though something is missing."

"It's as if my computer is a university!"

2. Comparisons

As and the way introduce clauses that express a direct comparison between actions or situations.

Examples:

"I'm thinking of joining a program as / the way a few of my friends have."

"Expect me to go to college the way they did."

3. Informal English

In informal spoken English, like is frequently used instead of as if / as though or as / the way.

Formal vs. Informal:

Formal: "It seems as though the job was made for me!"

Informal: "It seems like the job was made for me!"
