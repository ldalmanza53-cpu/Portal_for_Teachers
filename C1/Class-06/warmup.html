Grammar Foundation

When do we use Gerunds vs. Infinitives?

When talking about ways to meet new people, we rely on 4 main grammar structures. Review the formulas below:

Pattern A: Subject Gerund

Gerund (-ing) + Verb
Used as the topic/subject of a sentence.

"Joining a gym helps you meet active people."

Pattern B: Adjective + Infinitive

It is + Adj + Infinitive (to + verb)
Describes how easy/hard an activity is.

"It's easy to make friends at language exchanges."

Pattern C: Preposition + Gerund

Preposition (in/about/by) + -ing
After prepositions, always use a gerund.

"Are you interested in volunteering?"

Pattern D: Verb Patterns

Verb + Gerund / Infinitive
Specific verbs require a specific form.

"I decided to try networking apps." / "I enjoy attending workshops."

B1 Speaking & Discussion Prompts

Practice answering these discussion questions in pairs or groups. Use the target gerund and infinitive structures!

Prompt 1: Hobbies & Clubs

"Is joining a sports club or hobby group an effective way to meet people? Why or why not?"

Target starter: "I think joining a club is great for..."

Prompt 2: Online & Apps

"Do you enjoy using social networking apps to make new friends, or do you prefer meeting people in person?"

Target starter: "I prefer meeting people in person because..."

Prompt 3: Personal Advice

"What advice would you give to someone who finds it difficult to make new friends in a new city?"

Target starter: "I would advise them to start by..."

B1 Gerund & Infinitive Phrase Bank

Common Gerund Expressions (-ing):
  • Joining a sports team...
  • Attending local workshops...
  • Interested in volunteering...
  • Enjoy / Recommend going to events...
Common Infinitive Expressions (to + verb):
  • It is easy / difficult to break the ice.
  • A good way to make friends...
  • Decide to sign up for a class.
  • Refuse / Hope to connect with others.
