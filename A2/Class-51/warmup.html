Describing Recent Activities in Life

To talk about activities that happened in your recent past, English uses two main tenses: Present Perfect (focus on current relevance or recent time) and Past Simple (finished action at a specific finished time).

Present Perfect Tense

Have / Has + V3

Use this when the result is important NOW, or for very recent events without a specific finished time.

JUST (A short time ago)

"I have just finished my homework."

ALREADY (Sooner than expected)

"She has already cleaned her apartment."

YET (Negative & Questions)

"Have you called him yet?" / "I haven't called yet."

RECENTLY / LATELY

"They have recently started a new fitness routine."

Past Simple Tense

Verb-ed / V2

Use this when you mention a specific finished time in the past (e.g., yesterday, 2 hours ago, last night).

YESTERDAY

"I visited my grandparents yesterday."

LAST WEEK / MONTH / NIGHT

"We went to the gym last night."

... AGO (2 hours ago, 3 days ago)

"He bought a new bike two days ago."

SPECIFIC TIME / DATE

"I woke up at 7 AM this morning."

Quick Verb Form Comparison (A2 Essentials)

Base Form (V1) Past Simple (V2) Past Participle (V3) Example Present Perfect
clean cleaned cleaned I have just cleaned my room.
finish finished finished She has already finished work.
buy bought bought We have recently bought groceries.
do / make did / made done / made He hasn't done his chores yet.
go went gone / been They have just gone out.
