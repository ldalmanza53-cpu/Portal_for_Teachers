Reported Speech: Requests & Commands

Lesson Overview

How to Report Requests & Commands

When reporting requests or commands, use say to, tell, or ask followed by to + verb (infinitive) or not to + verb for negatives.

Grammar Reference Box (CD 3:34)

Original vs. Reported
Original Request (Direct)
"Arrive on time for the meeting."
Reported Request
She said to arrive on time for the meeting.
She told me to arrive on time for the meeting.
Original Negative Request
"Don't leave your bike in the hallway."
Reported Negative Request
He said not to leave my bike in the hallway.
He told me not to leave my bike in the hallway.
Polite Question Request
"Can you pick up some food on the way home?"
Reported Polite Request
She asked me to pick up some food.
1

TELL vs SAY

Tell requires an object pronoun (me, him, her, us, them). Say is followed directly by to + infinitive.

✔ He told me to come.

✔ He said to come.

✘ He told to come. (Incorrect)

2

Negative Commands

Change "Don't..." to not to + verb. Do not use don't or didn't in the reported part!

• Direct: "Don't shout!"

• Reported: She told us not to shout.

3

Asking with "Can you...?"

When someone asks a request starting with "Can you...?", report it using asked + person + to + verb. Drop "Can you".

• Direct: "Can you help me?"

• Reported: He asked me to help him.
