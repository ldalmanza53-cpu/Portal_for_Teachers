Obligations vs. Suggestions Toolkit

Learn how to express rules, prohibitions, choices, and recommendations when traveling around the world.

1. Strict Obligation

Rules, Laws & Absolute Necessity

must / have to

Use must or have to when an action is required by law, security, or authority.

"You must show your passport at immigration."
"Passengers have to pass through metal detectors."

2. Prohibition & Safety

Strictly Forbidden / Danger

mustn't / cannot

Use mustn't (must not) when something is forbidden, illegal, or dangerous.

"You mustn't leave your luggage unattended."
"Travelers mustn't carry liquids over 100ml."

3. Lack of Obligation

Optional / Not Necessary

don't have to

Use don't have to when an action is unnecessary. You can do it if you want, but it's optional!

"You don't have to print your ticket; mobile QR works."
"Tourists don't have to tip at local restaurants here."

4. Advice & Recommendations

Good Ideas & Tips

should / could / good idea

Use should, could, or it's a good idea to for friendly advice.

"You should pack comfortable walking shoes."
"It's a good idea to buy a regional SIM card."
