Core Rule

Statement Word Order in Indirect Questions

When asking for information politely, we use introductory phrases like "Do you know...", "Could you tell me...", or "Can you tell me...". The embedded Wh-clause switches from question word order to statement word order (Subject + Verb).

1. Verbs with 'Be'

Move 'be' after the subject.

✔ ...where the nearest ATM is?

✖ ...where is the nearest ATM?

2. Verbs with 'Do / Does'

Drop do/does. Add -s/-es for third person.

✔ ...what time the store opens?

✖ ...what time does the store open?

3. Modal Verbs ('Can')

Keep can, but place it after the subject.

✔ ...where I can catch the bus?

✖ ...where can I catch the bus?

🔹 Group 1: Wh-Questions with "Be" (is / are)
Singular Subject ("the nearest ATM") Where is the nearest ATM? Could you tell me where the nearest ATM is?
Plural Subject ("the restrooms") Where are the restrooms? Do you know where the restrooms are?
🔹 Group 2: Wh-Questions with Auxiliaries "Do / Does"
Plural Subject with "Do" ("the buses") How often do the buses run? Can you tell me how often the buses run?
Singular Subject with "Does" ("the bookstore") What time does the bookstore open? Do you know what time the bookstore opens?
🔹 Group 3: Wh-Questions with Modal "Can"
Modal Verb "Can" + Subject "I" Where can I catch the bus? Do you know where I can catch the bus?

Explanation

Indirect questions use statement order. Auxiliary verbs do and does are dropped, and singular subjects take -s on the main verb.

Rule 2: Verb 'Be' Placement
✅ ...where the ATM is?
❌ ...where is the ATM?
Tap for explanation
Explanation

In indirect questions, the verb is/are moves after the subject noun phrase: Subject + is/are.

Rule 3: Modal Verb 'Can'
✅ ...where I can rent a car?
❌ ...where can I rent a car?

Explanation

Modals like can are retained but follow subject-verb word order: Subject + can + base verb.
