Orders vs. Requests: Understanding the Difference

Learn when to use direct imperatives (orders) versus polite question forms (requests), and how to respond politely!

Direct Order ⚠️

Imperatives

Base Verb + Object. Direct command tone!

"Pass the salt!"
"Open your books."
"Bring the menu."
🚨 Usage Note: Used by teachers, bosses, or in emergencies. Can sound bossy or impolite with friends!
Informal Request 🤝

Can you...?

Can you + Base Verb + please?

"Can you pass the salt, please?"
"Can you help me?"
"Can you close the window?"
💡 Usage Note: Perfect for everyday friendly conversations with classmates, family, and friends!
Polite Request ✨

Could you please...?

Could you + please + Base Verb?

"Could you please bring the menu?"
"Could you open the door, please?"
"Could you please repeat that?"
🌟 Usage Note: Extra polite! Recommended in cafes, restaurants, hotels, and with strangers.
Responses 💬

Polite Answers

Saying YES or polite NO!

👍 Accepting:
  • "Sure! Here you go."
  • "Of course!"
  • "Yes, certainly."
👎 Polite Declining:
  • "I'm sorry, I can't right now."
  • "Sorry, I don't have one."
🔑 Tip: Always add "I'm sorry..." or "Sorry..." when saying NO to sound polite!
