Modal Requests vs. Gerund ("Would you mind") Comparison

Interchange SB-69 Rules
Request Type Grammar Formula Politeness Level Polite Agreement Polite Refusal
Can / Could / Would Modal + Base Verb Direct / Standard Polite "Sure, no problem!" / "I'd be glad to." "I'm really sorry, but I'm busy."
Would you mind (+ V-ing) Would you mind + Verb-ing Very Polite & Formal "No, not at all!" / "Not at all, I'd be happy to." "I'm sorry, I can't. I don't have time."
Would you mind NOT (+ V-ing) Would you mind NOT + Verb-ing Polite Negative Request "Oh, I'm sorry. I didn't know." "Actually, I need to finish this call."
Pitfall 1

No Infinitive "To" After Mind

Never put to + verb after "Would you mind". Always use gerund (-ing)!

Click to view example
Correct Syntax

❌ Would you mind to open the window?

✅ Would you mind opening the window?

Pitfall 2

Placement of "NOT"

Place NOT directly before the gerund: Would you mind NOT using...

Click to view example
Correct Syntax

❌ Would you mind to not smoke?

✅ Would you mind not smoking here?

Pitfall 3

Modals Require Base Verbs

Can, could, would must take base verb form (no -ing!).

Correct Syntax

❌ Could you lending me cash?

✅ Could you lend me twenty dollars?

Pitfall 4

Agreement Pragmatics

Saying "No" to "Would you mind" means you are HAPPY to help!

Agreement Rule
