Reviewing Past Events & Giving Suggestions

When we talk about past activities, trips, or projects in English, we use key functional patterns:
• Expressing Opinions: "I think the event was...", "In my opinion, it went well."
• Explaining Causes: "...because we arrived late", "...since it rained hard."
• Making Suggestions: "Next time, we should...", "They should have planned better."

💭

Card A: Expressing Opinions on Past Events

Opinions

Use these starting phrases to evaluate how a past activity turned out:

"I think the concert was exciting, but a bit too loud."
"In my opinion, the school trip was a huge success."
"I felt that the party was quite chaotic from the start."
"Overall, it was a disappointing outcome for everyone."
⚡

Card B: Expressing Reasons for Past Outcomes

Reasons

Connect the result of an event to its cause using linkers:

"...because we arrived late and missed the opening."
"...since the organizer forgot to send the invitation."
"...because the weather was terrible and it rained all day."
"...since nobody checked the schedule in advance."
💡

Card C: Suggestions for Improvements

Suggestions

Provide constructive suggestions using "should" or "it would be better":

"Next time, we should book the tickets early."
"They should have picked a much larger venue."
"It would be better to check the weather forecast first."
"We ought to assign team roles before starting."
🔍

Card D: Past Mistakes & Reflection Phrases

Reflections

Reflect on errors or regrets politely:

"It was a mistake to ignore the weather forecast."
"We shouldn't have waited until the very last minute."
"I regret not bringing warm jackets for everyone."
"In hindsight, communication could have been clearer."

💡 Fluency & Intonation Tips

  • 1. Pause at punctuation: Take a small breath after commas before introducing reasons with because or since.
  • 2. Emphasize key opinions: Stress strong adjectives like disappointing, fantastic, or chaotic.
  • 3. Clear suggestions: Use a warm, constructive tone when saying "Next time, we should..."
