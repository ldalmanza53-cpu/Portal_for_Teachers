Grammar Focus 1 Grammar Plus Ex. 1

1. Passive with by (Simple Past)

We use the Simple Past Passive when the object receiving the action is the main focus of history or discovery. Use was / were + past participle + by + person.

Regular Verbs (Same as Simple Past)

For regular verbs, the past participle ends in -ed.

Active: Leonardo da Vinci painted Mona Lisa in 1503.
Passive: Mona Lisa was painted by Leonardo da Vinci in 1503.
Irregular Verbs (Special Forms)

Some irregular verbs keep the simple past form; others change:

• build → built ("The Pyramids were built by the Egyptians.")
• write → written ("Pride and Prejudice was written by Jane Austen.")

2. Passive without by (Simple Present)

When it is obvious or not important who is doing the action (e.g. general people, workers, citizens), we omit by. Formula: is / are + past participle.

Why omit "by people"?

For global facts, industries, and habits, we don't say "by people" because it is obvious:

Example: "Both the Olympics and the World Cup are held every four years." (NOT: ... are held by people every four years.)
