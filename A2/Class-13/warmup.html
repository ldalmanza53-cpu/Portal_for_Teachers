Giving Options

3 Essential Patterns
We can either [A] or [B]...

Used to present two clear, distinct alternatives.

"We can either book a downtown conference hotel or rent a countryside retreat."
One alternative is to... while another is...

Formal structure for balanced comparison.

"One alternative is to hire freelance contractors, while another is to outsource."
Our primary choices boil down to...

Ideal for executive summaries & conclusions.

"Our primary choices boil down to reducing expenses or expanding sales."

Making Comparisons

3 Advanced Modifiers
Significantly / Far more [adj.] than...

Emphasizes large differences in quality or cost.

"The electric fleet is significantly more cost-effective in the long run than diesel."
Not nearly as [adj.] as...

Negative comparison emphasizing deficit.

"The remote option is not nearly as interactive as an in-person workshop."
While [A] offers [X], [B] provides...

Concessive contrast for trade-off analysis.

"While the city offers superior Wi-Fi, the resort provides better relaxation."

Evaluating & Deciding

3 Decision Phrases
Taking all factors into account...

Holistic intro connector before final verdict.

"Taking all factors into account, the hybrid model is the most viable."
The main drawback of [A] is [B], which outweighs...

Weighting drawbacks against benefits.

"The main drawback of Option A is high cost, which outweighs its perks."
We strongly recommend [A] because it strikes the best balance...

Persuasive recommendation phrasing.

"We strongly recommend Option 1 as it strikes the best balance."
