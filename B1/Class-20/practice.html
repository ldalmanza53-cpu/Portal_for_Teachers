Expressing Cultural Expectations

In English, we use specific expressions with infinitive verbs (to + base verb) to talk about cultural rules, habits, etiquette, and social obligations.

Sentence Structure Pattern

1. Time / Condition Clause
"When you visit someone..."
"If someone sends a gift..."
2. Expectation Expression
you're expected to
it's the custom to
3. Base Verb (Infinitive)
"...bring a small gift."
"...thank the person."
Tradition / Habit

it's the custom to + [verb]

Used for traditional actions or long-standing cultural habits.

"When you visit someone, it's the custom to bring a small gift."
Social Expectation

you're expected to + [verb]

Reflects general social obligations or what society anticipates you will do.

"If you bring friends, you're expected to ask first."
General Rule

you're supposed to + [verb]

Refers to rules, guidelines, or duties people should follow.

"You're supposed to check with the host first."
Prohibition

you aren't supposed to + [verb]

Indicates actions that are discouraged or against etiquette.

"You aren't supposed to arrive too early."
Permissibility

it's (not) acceptable to + [verb]

Indicates whether a behavior is polite or socially allowed.

"It's acceptable to split the bill with friends."
Quick Check

Which structure fits best?

"In many countries, _______ take off your shoes when entering a home."
