📚 Passive Modals Matrix & Disagreement Rules

Essential grammar guide for Cambridge Interchange Level 3, Unit 15: Giving recommendations, opinions, and expressing polite disagreement.

💡 When you think something is a GOOD IDEA

Recommendation

Use these passive modal structures when giving a opinion or suggestion that is advisable, but not strictly enforced by law or severe necessity.

Positive Recommendation: should + be + past participle

e.g. Clubs should be required to install soundproof walls.

Negative Recommendation: shouldn't + be + past participle

e.g. Pet owners shouldn't be allowed to walk dogs without a leash.

Formal / Alternative Suggestion: ought (not) to + be + past participle

e.g. People ought (not) to be required to wear a helmet.

⚡ When you think something is ABSOLUTELY NECESSARY

Strong Obligation

Use these strong passive modal structures when expressing strict necessity, laws, or mandatory regulations.

Strong Law / Command: must + be + past participle

e.g. A law must be passed to control vehicle pollution.

Prohibition: mustn't + be + past participle

e.g. Riding on sidewalk mustn't be permitted.

Rule Requirement / Informal Necessity: has / have to be | has / have got to be + V3

e.g. A rule has to be made. Something has got to be done.

⚠️ B1 / B2 Common Passive Modal Pitfalls

Pitfall #1: Forgetting the auxiliary 'be'
❌ Laws must pass to protect privacy.
✔️ Laws must be passed to protect privacy.
Pitfall #2: Base verb instead of Past Participle
❌ People shouldn't be allow to use...
✔️ People shouldn't be allowed to use...
Pitfall #3: Incorrect 'ought' preposition placement
❌ People ought be required...
✔️ People ought to be required...
