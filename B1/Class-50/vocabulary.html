Cambridge Interchange Level 3 • Grammar Focus

Describing Purpose in Business & Everyday Goals

Learn how to state business objectives and recommendations using Infinitive Clauses (To... / In order to...) and Infinitive Clauses with 'For' (For [noun] to...).

1 Direct Infinitive Clause

To / (In order to) + Base Verb

Use an infinitive phrase at the start of a sentence to state an action goal. In order is optional but adds formal emphasis.

"To attract talented professionals, a company should offer competitive salaries."
"(In order) to finance a new business, it's a good idea to get a bank loan."
2 Clause with Subject 'For'

For + Noun/Object + To + Base Verb

When specifying the subject or entity achieving the goal (e.g., an app, a restaurant, a health club), place For [Noun] before the infinitive.

"For a small company to be profitable, it should have a good marketing plan."
"(In order) for an app to succeed, it has to be easy to use."

Common B1 Student Pitfalls to Avoid

Watch Out!
Don't use "For + -ing" for purpose of actions

❌ For attracting customers, you need a location...

✅ To attract customers... OR For a business to attract...

Don't forget 'to' after the noun

❌ For an app succeeds, it must be clean...

✅ For an app to succeed, it must be clean...

Don't omit 'for' when inserting a noun

❌ In order a company to grow...

✅ In order for a company to grow...
