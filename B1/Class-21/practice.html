Target Grammar Concept

Omitting Relative Pronouns & Inverting Sentences

The relative pronouns who or that can be left out in noun phrases as subjects or objects. You can also reverse the sentence structure without changing the meaning!

Pattern Form A

Starting with the Noun Phrase

[Noun Phrase] + [Subject + Relative Clause (No 'who/that')] + [is/are] + [Complement]
  • With relative pronoun: One thing that I'd be nervous about is getting lost.
  • Omitted (Standard): One thing I'd be nervous about is getting lost.
  • Plural Example: Two things I'd be interested in are the food and music.
Pattern Form B (Inverted)

Starting with the Complement

[Complement] + [is/are] + [Noun Phrase] + [Subject + Relative Clause (No 'who/that')]
  • With relative pronoun: Getting lost is one thing that I'd be nervous about.
  • Omitted (Standard): Getting lost is one thing I'd be nervous about.
  • Plural Example: The food and music are two things I'd be interested in.
