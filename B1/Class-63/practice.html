📚 Grammar Cheat Sheet & Punctuation Matrix

Essential reference for Cambridge Interchange Level 3, Unit 12: Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses & Punctuation Rules.

🎯 Defining Relative Clauses

Essential Info

These clauses give essential information to identify which person or thing you are talking about. Without this clause, the sentence is incomplete or unclear.

Rule for People: person / someone + WHO / THAT
Rule for Things: thing / object + WHICH / THAT
Punctuation Rule: NO COMMAS! 🚫
Examples:
  • My best friend is a person who always listens to me.
  • My mother is someone that inspires me every day.
  • I like people who have a good sense of humor.

💬 Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Extra / Optional Info

These clauses give extra, non-essential information. The person is already clearly identified (e.g., by name or relationship). The sentence still makes full sense without the clause.

Rule for People: Name / Relationship + , WHO ... ,
Relative Pronoun Restriction: NEVER USE 'THAT'! ONLY USE 'WHO' OR 'WHICH'
Punctuation Rule: MUST USE COMMAS AROUND CLAUSE ( , ... , )
Examples:
  • My neighbor, who lives on the third floor, always plays loud music.
  • My English teacher, who studied at Oxford, is very strict.
  • My sister, who works at a hospital, travels a lot.

⚠️ Common Grammar Traps (B1 / B2 Level Pitfalls)

Trap #1: Using 'that' after a comma
❌ My uncle, that lives in NY, is a doctor.
✔️ My uncle, who lives in NY, is a doctor.
Trap #2: Missing the closing comma
❌ My neighbor, who loves dogs plays piano.
✔️ My neighbor, who loves dogs, plays piano.
Trap #3: Unnecessary commas in defining clauses
❌ A person, who works hard, succeeds.
✔️ A person who works hard succeeds.
