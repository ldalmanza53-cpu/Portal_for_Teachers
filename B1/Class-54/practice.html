📊 Expression Matrix: Purpose vs. Reason Structures

B1/B2 Mastery
Expression Function Followed By Example Sentence Key Tip
In order for [X] to be [Y], Purpose / Requirement it has to / needs to + Verb In order for a store to be successful, it has to be open 24/7. Do not omit "for" or "to be".
To [Verb], Short Purpose [Subject] must / needs to + Verb To attract clients, a beach hotel must offer ocean views. Simplified form of "In order to".
because Reason (Clause) Subject + Verb It's successful because their comic books are cheap. Always requires a full clause.
because of / due to Reason (Noun) Noun Phrase / Gerund It's popular due to its great customer service. Never put a verb clause directly after.
The reason... is that Formal Reason Subject + Verb The reason it's successful is that they host games. Use "is that", avoid "is because".
famous for Reputation Factor Noun / Verb-ing The juice bar is famous for serving organic dragonfruit. Pairs with gerund (-ing) for actions.
⚠️ Common Trap #1

Double Preposition Confusion

Incorrect: "In order for to succeed..."
Correct: "In order to succeed..." OR "In order for a store to succeed..."

⚠️ Common Trap #2

Mixing Clause vs Noun

Incorrect: "It's popular due to it has low prices."
Correct: "It's popular due to its low prices." OR "...because it has low prices."

⚠️ Common Trap #3

Redundant "Is Because"

Incorrect: "The reason is because it's cheap."
Correct: "The reason is that it's cheap." OR "It is successful because it is cheap."
