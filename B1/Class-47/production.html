Cambridge Interchange • Unit 11 Grammar Focus

Expressing Regret & Hypothetical Situations

Master how to talk about past decisions you wish you had made differently (should have) and imaginary past consequences (would/could have).

1 Expressing Regret

Should have + Past Participle

Use should have (or shouldn't have) + V3 to express regret about past actions you did or didn't do.

"I should have done an internship in college."
"I shouldn't have taken out a student loan."
2 Probable Outcome

Would have + Past Participle

Use would have + V3 in third conditionals to describe definite or highly probable hypothetical results in the past.

"If I'd listened, I would have taken courses."
3 Possible Outcome

Could have + Past Participle

Use could have + V3 to express potential possibilities or abilities that were achievable in a hypothetical past.

"If I hadn't been irresponsible, I could have gotten better grades."

The Third Conditional Structure

Imaginary Past Condition ➔ Imaginary Past Result
Condition Clause (If-clause)
If + Subject + had / hadn't + V3

Example: "If I had listened to my financial advisor..."

Result Clause (Main clause)
Subject + would / could have + V3

Example: "...I wouldn't have borrowed money for a new car."
