Grammar Focus • Reference Chart

Two Ways to Structure Your Sentences

Notice how relative pronouns like that and who connect ideas. They can often be omitted in informal speech!

Structure 1 Fronted Relative Clause Focus

Pattern: [Noun Phrase + (that/who) + Clause] + is/are + [Thing/Person]

Something (that) I'd be worried about is the local food.

Target: Feelings / Anxieties

One thing (that) I'd really miss is hanging out with my friends.

Target: Activities / Habits

The people (who/that) I'd miss the most are my parents.

Target: Plural People (Use 'are')
Structure 2 Direct Subject / Thing Focus

Pattern: [Thing/Person] + is/are + [Noun Phrase + (that/who) + Clause]

The local food is something (that) I'd be worried about.

Target: Subject fronting

Hanging out with my friends is one thing (that) I'd really miss.

Target: Gerund phrase fronting

My parents are the people (who/that) I'd miss the most.

Target: Plural subject fronting
💡
Grammar Rule Tip Use who or that for people, and that or which for things. When followed by a subject + verb (e.g., "I'd miss"), the relative pronoun is optional!
