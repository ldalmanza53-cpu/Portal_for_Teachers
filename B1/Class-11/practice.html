Grammar Rules & Patterns

Indirect Requests Reference Guide

Learn how direct statements, imperatives, yes/no questions, and Wh- questions shift when conveyed indirectly through a messenger.

1. Indirect Yes/No Questions
Ask + person + if / whether + Subject + Verb

Direct: "Mary, did you get my message about your phone?"

Indirect: "Could you ask Mary if she got my message about her phone?"

2. Indirect Wh- Questions
Ask + person + Wh-word + Subject + Verb

Direct: "Ms. Martin, when is our assignment due?"

Indirect: "Could you ask Ms. Martin when our assignment is due?"

⚠️ Embedded Word Order: Subject comes BEFORE verb (no inversion)!
3. Indirect Imperatives / Requests
Ask / Tell + person + to + base verb

Direct: "Mary, please return my call when you get your phone back."

Indirect: "Could you ask Mary to return my call when she gets her phone back?"

4. Indirect Statements
Tell + person + (that) + Subject + Verb

Direct: "Ms. Martin, I won't be in class tomorrow night."

Indirect: "Could you tell Ms. Martin that I won't be in class tomorrow night?"

¡Atención Hispanohablantes! Spanish vs. English Traps
1. Pronoun & Possessive Shifts

Remember to change pronouns to match the recipient: "your phone" → "her phone", "you get" → "she gets".

2. No Auxiliary Inversion

In Spanish "cuándo es la tarea" keeps inversion. In English indirect questions, use statement word order: "when our assignment is due" (NOT "when is...").

3. "Ask" vs. "Tell" Distinction

Use ASK for questions or requested actions ("Ask Mary if..."). Use TELL for statements/messages ("Tell Ms. Martin that...").
