A2 Grammar Spotlight

Making Direct vs. Indirect Requests

In English, we change how we ask for things depending on who we are talking to and how polite we need to be!

🗣️
⚡

Direct Requests

Casual & Fast

Used with friends, family, or quick everyday interactions. They are short and clear. Always say "please"!

imperative + please

"Pass me the salt, please."

Can you...?

"Can you open the window?"

✨

Indirect Requests

Very Polite & Formal

Used with strangers, teachers, bosses, or in formal places (hotels, banks, cafes).

Could you tell me...?

"Could you tell me where the station is?"

Would you mind...?

"Would you mind turning down the music?"

Talking to a Close Friend / Sibling

DIRECT REQUEST OK

When you know someone very well, you can use direct requests. Keep them friendly with "Can you...?" or "Please".

Example Request:

"Can you give me a pencil?"

💡 Word Order Spotlight (Crucial A2 Rule!)

Notice how the subject and verb change places when we make an indirect request!

Direct Question
Where is the bank?

Verb ("is") comes BEFORE the subject ("the bank").

Polite Indirect Request
Could you tell me where the bank is?

Subject ("the bank") comes BEFORE the verb ("is")!

❌ Common Mistake: "Could you tell me where is the bank?"
✅ Correct: "Could you tell me where the bank is?"
