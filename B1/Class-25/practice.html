Grammar Plus Unit 6 Summary

Describing Problems & Specifying Required Repairs

Learn two essential grammar competencies: (1) Describing physical flaws using nouns with has/have vs. participles/adjectives with is/are, and (2) Specifying repair solutions using need + passive infinitive vs. need + gerund.

Part 1 Focus

1. Describing Problems 1

You can describe a flaw with a noun phrase (has/have a + noun) or an adjective / past participle (is/are + participle).

Participle Formation Note:
• Regular verbs (past & past participle are identical): I chipped the vase. → The vase is chipped.
• Irregular verbs (different past & participle): I tore my jacket. → My jacket is torn.
Noun Structure (has / have) Adjective / Participle (is / are)
These jeans have a tear in them. They are stained.
The door has a dent in it. The back light is broken.
This table has some damage on top. The wood is scratched.
There is a hole in the blouse. It is leaking. / It has a chip.
Part 2 Focus

2. Describing Problems 2

When describing what needs repair, two equivalent structures are commonly used depending on speaker perspective:

Speaker A Rule: Need + Passive Infinitive
Subject + need / needs + to be + Past Participle

"The oven needs to be fixed." "The windows need to be washed."

NOT: "The oven needs to be fixing." (Must use past participle after 'to be')
Speaker B Rule: Need + Gerund
Subject + need / needs + Verb-ing

"First, the walls need painting." "It needs shampooing."
