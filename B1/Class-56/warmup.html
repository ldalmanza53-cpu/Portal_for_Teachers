Must Have + Past Participle (V3)

Used when existing physical or digital evidence points to only one logical explanation in the past.

// Formula Structure
Subject + must have + Verb (V3 / Past Participle)
Forensic Example #1: "The server room temperature dropped below zero; the cooling system must have malfunctioned during the night sync."
Forensic Example #2: "Looking at the encrypted log files, an administrator must have authorized the high-privilege override key."
Never use "must of" Positive Logical Deduction
90 - 100% Impossibility TAG: #ImpossibilityNegative

Can't / Couldn't Have + V3

Used when physical or circumstantial evidence renders a past action logically impossible.

// Formula Structure
Subject + can't / couldn't have + Verb (V3)
Forensic Example #1: "The suspect couldn't have bypassed the biometric scanner; his access keycard was registered in Tokyo at that exact hour."
Forensic Example #2: "The database leak can't have originated from HR, because their department lacks SQL write privileges."
Avoid "must not have" for impossibility Negative Impossibility
30 - 50% Plausible Possibility TAG: #HypothesisUncertain

May / Might / Could Have + V3

Used when several competing theories exist and the evidence is incomplete or inconclusive.

// Formula Structure
Subject + may / might / could have + Verb (V3)
Forensic Example #1: "The target file might have been intercepted during the unencrypted transit window."
Forensic Example #2: "The malware could have executed via a corrupted email attachment opened by staff."
Might = lower probability Speculative Hypothesis
