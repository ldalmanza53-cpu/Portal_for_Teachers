Grammar Plus 2 • Giving Reasons

Clause Connectors vs Noun Phrase Prepositions

Learn how to choose between because / since (+ Subject + Verb clause) vs because of / due to / for (+ Noun phrase).

Clause Connectors (+ Subject + Verb)

Because / Since introduce an adverb clause with a full verb phrase:
"Since I was only a few blocks away, I went to Martin's."

Prepositions & Noun Connectors (+ Noun Object)

Because of / Due to / For are followed directly by a noun phrase object:
"Shops are closing because of the construction of malls."

Grammar Focus Connector Required Following Structure Example Sentence
Direct Purpose To / In order to + Base Verb "To run a boutique, you need passion."
Purpose with Subject For / In order for + Noun Object + To + Verb "For a website to succeed, it must be accurate."
Reason (Clause) Because / Since + Subject + Verb Clause "Since I was downtown, I visited Martin's."
Reason (Noun Phrase) Because of / Due to / For + Noun Phrase / Object "Shops close due to mall construction."
Reason (Explanation) The reason why + S + V + is to + Verb "The reason why people shop there is to save."
Pitfall 1: "For + Infinitive"

❌ For to start a salon...
✅ To start a salon...

Pitfall 2: "Because of + S + V"

❌ Because of prices are high...
✅ Because prices are high...

Pitfall 3: Omission of 'For'

❌ In order an app to survive...
✅ In order for an app to survive...
