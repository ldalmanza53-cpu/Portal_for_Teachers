Relative Pronouns: Subject vs. Object

We use relative pronouns like who and that to join sentences and describe people. Discover when you MUST keep them and when you CAN DROP them!

1

As Subject of a Clause

REQUIRED ⚠️

The relative pronoun acts as the subject of the relative clause (followed directly by a VERB). You cannot omit it!

I like people who / that aren't too serious.

Rule: "who/that" is the subject for verb "aren't".

I like people who / that have a good sense of humor.

Rule: "who/that" is the subject for verb "have".

2

As Object of a Clause

OPTIONAL ✂️

The relative clause already has its own SUBJECT before the verb. You can omit (leave out) who/that!

I want someone (who / that) I can have fun with.

Rule: "I" is the subject, so "who/that" is optional.

I'd like someone (who / that) I can talk to easily.

Rule: "I" is the subject, so "who/that" is optional.
