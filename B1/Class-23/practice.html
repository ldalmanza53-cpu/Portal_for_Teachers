Two Distinct Ways to Describe Object Defects

When reporting damage or problems with items, English speakers use two interchangeable grammatical formulas: Past Participles acting as adjectives, or Noun Phrases!

Pattern 1: Past Participle

Subject + [be] + Past Participle

Subject + is / are + Past Participle

Use the past participle form of the verb as a descriptive adjective right after the verb be (is/are).

• "This pitcher is cracked."
• "My car is dented."
• "The shirt is torn." Irregular!
Pattern 2: Noun Phrase

Subject + [have] + a/an + Noun

Subject + has / have + (a/an) Noun

Use the noun form after the verb have (has/have). Include an article (a/an) for countable nouns!

• "It has a crack."
• "My car has a dent."
• "The ceiling has damage." Uncountable: No 'a'!
