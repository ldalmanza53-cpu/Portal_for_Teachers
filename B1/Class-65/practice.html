📚 Tag Questions Matrix & Intonation Rules

Complete grammar guide for Cambridge Interchange Level 3, Unit 13: Tag questions for giving opinions, seeking confirmation, and expressing social agreement.

🔄 1. The Polarity Flip & Auxiliary Rule

Core Formula

Tag questions mirror the auxiliary verb or modal of the main clause in opposite polarity. If there is no auxiliary (simple present), use do / does / don't / doesn't.

Positive Clause ➔ Negative Tag: Vandalism makes (+), doesn't it (-)?

Simple present verb 'makes' requires 'does' ➔ negative tag 'doesn't it?'.

Negative Clause ➔ Positive Tag: There aren't (-) enough shelters, are there (+)?

Negative verb 'aren't' flips to positive 'are there?'.

Modal Verbs (can / should): Teachers can't (-)... can they (+)? / Government should (+)... shouldn't it (-)?

🗣️ 2. Pronoun Conversion & Intonation

Pronouns & Voice

The pronoun in the tag always replaces the subject noun phrase. Intonation changes whether you are giving an opinion or asking a genuine question.

Subject Noun Mapping:

• The government ➔ it
• Overcrowded classrooms / Teachers ➔ they
• There is / There are ➔ there

Falling Intonation ↘️ (Seeking Confirmation):

You expect the listener to agree with your opinion. (e.g. "Bullying is a problem, ↘️ isn't it?")

Rising Intonation ↗️ (Asking Real Question):

You are unsure and asking for actual information. (e.g. "You don't live near here, ↗️ do you?")

⚠️ B1 / B2 Common Tag Question Traps

Pitfall #1: Special Case "I am"
❌ I am late, am not I?
✔️ I am late, aren't I?
Pitfall #2: Repeat Noun in Tag
❌ Vandalism is bad, isn't vandalism?
✔️ Vandalism is bad, isn't it?
Pitfall #3: Forgetting "Do / Does" in Simple Present
❌ It makes sense, isn't it?
✔️ It makes sense, doesn't it?
