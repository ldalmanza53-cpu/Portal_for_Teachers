Formula 1: Adjective

It is + [Adjective] + to + [Verb]

It is + traditional / customary + to + [verb base]

Use the adjective forms (traditional, customary) right after It is when introducing a general rule.

• "It is traditional to have a large meal at noon."
• "It is customary for people to take public transit."
Formula 2: Passive Expectation

Subject + [be] + [Participle/Adj] + to + [Verb]

You are / Are you + expected / supposed + to + [verb]

Use the past participle / adjective forms ending in -ed (expected, supposed) after the verb be (are/is/was).

• "You are expected to kiss people on both cheeks."
• "Are you supposed to arrive early?"
Formula 3: Noun Phrases

[Article / Determiner] + [Noun] + of

a / the / any + tradition / custom / expectations + of

Use the noun forms after articles (a, the) or determiners (any, many).

• "My family has a tradition of eating dessert."
• "What is the custom of greeting people?"
• "I didn't have any expectations."
Formula 4: Main Verb

Auxiliary + Subject + [Base Verb]

do you / did you + suppose / expect + [clause]

Use the base verb form when acting as the main action after auxiliary verbs like do / does / did.

• "What do you suppose I should do?"
• "I didn't expect him to arrive so late."

Part B Pair Work

Compare Expectations with a Partner 🗣️

Take turns interviewing your partner about cultural expectations using the prompt cards below.

🇲🇽

Mexico Context

"It is traditional to have a large meal at noon in Mexico."

Ask partner: "What time is it customary to have the main meal in your country?"
🇧🇷

Brazil Context

"You are expected to kiss people on both cheeks in Brazil."

Ask partner: "How are you supposed to greet friends and strangers in your culture?"
🇵🇪

Peru Context

"It is customary for people to take public transportation in Peru."

Ask partner: "Is it customary to use public transport, or do most people drive?"
🍽️

Dining Customs

"My family has a tradition of eating dessert at every meal."

Ask partner: "What do you suppose a visitor should do when they want more food?"
