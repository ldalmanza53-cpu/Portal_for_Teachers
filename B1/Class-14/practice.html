Past Continuous vs. Simple Past Rules

Learn when to use the ongoing background past continuous versus interrupting simple past events.

1. Past Continuous (Ongoing Action)
was / were + Verb-ing

Use the past continuous for an ongoing action in the past (background activity).

  • While I was working out, I calculated how many calories I burned.
  • As scientists were doing research, they discovered that women need more sleep.
  • A man was looking for his cat when he found a package.
2. Simple Past (Interrupting Event)
Regular Verb-ed / Irregular Past

Use the simple past for an event that interrupts or completes during that ongoing action.

  • While I was working out, I calculated calories.
  • As scientists were doing research, they discovered a fact.
  • A man was looking for his cat when he found a package.

Time Clause Connectors: While / As vs. When

Connector Grammar Structure Following Example Sentence
While / As Past Continuous (was/were + -ing) While she was sleeping, the floor collapsed.
When Simple Past (verb-ed / irregular) Jason was walking home when he saw a puppy.
¡Atención Hispanohablantes! Pretérito Imperfecto vs. Indefinido
1. Ongoing = Past Continuous

In Spanish, "Mientras caminaba..." (Imperfecto) expresses ongoing action. In English, always use "While I was walking...".

2. Interruption = Simple Past

The interrupting action in Spanish is Pretérito Indefinido (e.g., "...cuando vi un perrito"). Translate this as Simple Past: "...when I saw a puppy".

3. Avoid Common Mistakes

❌ Don't say: "When I was driving, I was having an accident."
✅ Correct: "While I was driving, I had an accident."
