Grammar Rules & Structural Breakdown

VRB-75 Indirect Request Structures

Master the exact verb patterns used when asking favors indirectly in polite B1 spoken English.

1. Would you mind + Gerund (-ing)
Would you mind + Verb-ing...?

Example: "Would you mind taking in the mail every day?"

❌ Incorrect: "Would you mind take in..." OR "...to take in..."
2. Could you + Base Verb
Could you + Base Form of Verb...?

Example: "And could you feed the fish?"

❌ Incorrect: "Could you feeding..." OR "Could you to feed..."
3. I was wondering if you could...
I was wondering if you could + Base Verb...?

Example: "I was wondering if you could water the plants?"

Very tentative and polite request frame.
4. I was wondering if you'd mind...
I was wondering if you'd mind + Verb-ing...?

Example: "I was wondering if you'd mind walking the dog?"

'you'd mind' = 'you would mind' (requires -ing).
5. Is it OK if + Subject + Present Simple Verb...?
Is it OK if + Subject (he/she/it/name) + Verb-S (Present Simple)...?

Example: "Is it OK if Megan spends the weekend?"

⚠️ Notice the 3rd person singular -s suffix ("Megan spends", "he needs", "she visits").
¡Atención Hispanohablantes! Key Pragmatic & Grammatical Traps
1. Mind + Gerund (-ing)

Translating "¿Te importaría alimentar...?" leads students to use infinitive (*"to feed"*). In English, mind always requires the gerund: "Would you mind feeding...?".

2. 3rd Person Suffix (-s)

In "Is it OK if Megan [spend]...", do not leave the verb in base form! Because Megan = She, you must add -s: "Is it OK if Megan spends...?".

3. Responding to "Would you mind...?"

To AGREE to a favor, answer in the negative: "Not at all" or "No problem!" (meaning "No, I do NOT mind helping you!"). Saying *"Yes"* means you refuse!
