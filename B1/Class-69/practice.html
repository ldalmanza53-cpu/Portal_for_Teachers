📖 Unit 16 Grammar Reference Matrix: Future Goals

Master the exact textbook rules for describing goals and possible future accomplishments using Future Perfect (hope I'll have + V3) and Infinitive Perfect (would like to have + V3).

⚖️ 1. Future Perfect vs Infinitive Perfect Comparison

🔵 Future Perfect (Hopes & Expectations) hope I'll HAVE + V3
Question: What do you hope you'll have achieved?

Used when expressing personal expectations or optimism about completed future milestones by a specific time.

  • By this time next year, I hope I'll have finished my degree.
  • By the time I'm 60, I hope I'll have retired comfortably.
🟣 Infinitive Perfect (Desires & Aspirations) would like to HAVE + V3
Question: What would you like to have achieved?

Used when expressing wishes, desires, or personal aspirations for future accomplishment.

  • Three years from now, I'd like to have bought a car.
  • In 10 years, I'd like to have built my own company.

⚠️ Pitfall Alert: Common Grammar Traps

Trap #1: Using Base Verb instead of V3
❌ I hope I'll have finish my work.
✔️ I hope I'll have finished my work.
After 'have', you MUST always use the Past Participle (V3)!
Trap #2: Using "has" with third-person future
❌ She hopes she'll has saved money.
✔️ She hopes she'll have saved money.
Modal 'will' and infinitive 'to' are always followed by base 'have', NEVER 'has'!
Trap #3: Omitting 'have' entirely
❌ I'd like to bought a house.
✔️ I'd like to have bought a house.
Without 'have', it is standard infinitive: "I'd like to buy..."
