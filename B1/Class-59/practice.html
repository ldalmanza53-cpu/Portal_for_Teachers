📐 Passive Process Grammar Matrix

In English, when describing a multi-step process or procedure (such as making a movie), we use the passive voice because the action and the object receiving the action are more important than who performs it.

1️⃣ Present Passive

is / are + Past Participle (V3)

Used for standard, routine steps in a procedure. Singular objects take is; plural objects take are.

Singular: A screenplay is chosen.
Plural: Many drawings are presented.
Agent (by): Movement is added to scenes by animators.

2️⃣ Modal & Semi-Modal Passive

modal + BE + Past Participle (V3)

Used when describing possibility, necessity, requirement, or obligation in a step. The base verb BE always stays unchanged after modals.

have to / need to: Characters have to be drawn.
might / may: Drawings might be changed 10 times.
must / can: Technical directors must be hired.

⚠️ 3 Common B1 Passive Process Traps To Avoid

1. Omitting 'BE' After Modals

❌ "The drawings might changed."

✔️ "The drawings might be changed."

Modals must always be followed by 'be' before the V3 participle.

2. Using V2 Instead of V3

❌ "First, storyboards are drew."

✔️ "First, storyboards are drawn."

'Draw' is irregular: draw (V1) → drew (V2) → drawn (V3).

3. Singular/Plural Mismatch

❌ "The scenes is populated."

✔️ "The scenes are populated."

'Scenes' is plural, so use 'are'. Singular nouns take 'is'.
